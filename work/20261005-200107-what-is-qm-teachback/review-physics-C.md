## Issues
- [minor] "amplitudes, unlike chances, can be negative, so they can cancel" — true for the real polarization amplitudes used here. In general, though, amplitudes are complex numbers, and the rule is magnitude squared, not "squared". A learner who keeps "negative or positive numbers" will have to unlearn it when phases appear. Fix: "amplitudes, unlike chances, can be negative (in general they carry a phase, like a direction on a dial), so they can cancel."
- [minor] "measuring a thing generally changes it" — the closing summary could pull the learner back toward "uncertainty = measurement disturbance", which paragraph 35 correctly rules out. The physics is fine (a filter physically re-prepares the photon). Fix: keep it, and add "and some pairs of properties can never both be sharp, whatever we do" so that the two ideas stay separate in the takeaway.
- [minor] "Position and momentum ... are another such pair" — "such pair" comes right after the 50/50 rule. The pairing is right (no state is sharp in both), but position and momentum don't split 50/50: the more sharply one is defined, the more spread out the other is. Fix: "another incompatible pair: the sharper one is, the more spread out the other must be."

## Sound points worth keeping
- Step 3 rules out only the specific "secretly V or H" picture and claims nothing more (no overreach toward Bell). Superposition is framed as "one definite direction described using two others", not "both at once". The limit of the compass analogy is stated explicitly.
- The arithmetic is correct: 0.71² ≈ 0.5, the three-filter answer is 1/8 and the check question's answer is 1/8. The classical-wave/Malus caveat (that the quantum part is "photon by photon") is accurate. "Observation" is defined as a physical record. Interpretations are left open, and the text says outright that decoherence doesn't settle the measurement problem.
