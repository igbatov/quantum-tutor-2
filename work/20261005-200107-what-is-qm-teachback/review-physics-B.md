## Issues
- [minor] "So each pattern has one definite energy" — the "no volume control" argument is a fair gloss, but normalization isn't the deeper reason energy is fixed per pattern. For a stationary state the energy is set by the pattern itself and doesn't depend on the wave's size at all. At this level it does no harm. Fix (optional): add "and in quantum mechanics a pattern's energy is set by its shape alone (its rate of oscillation, via E = hf), not by how big the wave is."
- [minor] "Nothing lies below it, so the electron has nowhere lower to go" — this answers "why no lower energy?" but not the classical worry raised earlier, that an orbiting charge radiates all the time. Fix: one clause such as "and a steady pattern doesn't change over time, so unlike an orbiting charge it doesn't keep giving off light."
- [minor] "it can't say when a particular excited atom will drop, only the chances" — this is true of the standard theory. Some interpretations (e.g. pilot-wave) are deterministic underneath, though they predict no better in practice. Paragraph 30 already flags that interpretations are debated, so this is acceptable as written. Fix (optional): "as far as anyone can tell, only the chances."

- Physics is accurate. Hydrogen Balmer lines (656/486/434/410 nm), the ~10^-11 s classical collapse time, E = hf with h, the Lyman lines in the UV, rungs crowding toward 0 in −13.6/n² eV, and the quantum-dot check question (smaller means bluer) are all right.
- The string analogy's limits are stated explicitly. Uncertainty is framed as a wave property, not measurement clumsiness. "Observing" means any interaction that leaves a record. Decoherence is mentioned without claiming it solves measurement, and interpretations are handled even-handedly.
