# Check your understanding

Answer in your own words. A few sentences each is plenty. No math needed.

**1.** Imagine a friend who has never heard of quantum mechanics asks what it's about. In two or three sentences, tell them what quantum mechanics describes and what makes it different from the physics of everyday objects like balls and planets.

**2.** A lab sends single particles of light (photons) into a device, one at a time. Inside, each photon has two possible routes, and the routes join up again before reaching two detectors, A and B. With both routes open, every photon ends up at detector A. Detector B never clicks.

Now someone puts a block in one of the two routes, so photons on that route are stopped. Predict what happens at detector B, and explain why. (Hint: think about what having two open routes was doing.)

**3.** A friend says: "Quantum uncertainty only exists because our measuring tools are too clumsy. Every time we look at an electron we bump it. If engineers built gentle enough instruments, we could know exactly where an electron is and exactly how fast it's moving, both at once." Do you agree? Explain why or why not.

**4.** In question 2, quantum mechanics tells us exactly how likely each detector is to click. Does it also tell us what the photon is "really doing" on its way through the device when nobody is checking which route it takes? Is that question settled among physicists?
