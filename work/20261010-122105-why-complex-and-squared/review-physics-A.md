## Issues
- [major] "each arm's amplitude must be a pair of real numbers ... that is a complex number" (opening; repeated in "How they relate": "the pair is forced by the one-arm plate"). This overstates what the experiment shows. The plate argument rules out one real number per arm and shows the plate rotates whatever describes that arm. It does not show "exactly a pair". Quaternionic amplitudes (four reals per arm) pass every count in (a) to (d), and they are constrained only by other experiments (Peres 1979 proposal; Kaiser, George & Werner 1984 neutron test; Procopio et al. 2017 photons). The opening also says nothing of the Stueckelberg equivalence that the later section concedes. Fix: "so one real number per arm is not enough: each arm needs at least two, and the plate turns them like a clock hand. The simplest such object is a complex number. Larger choices such as quaternions pass this test too and are ruled out by other experiments, and for a single system a real theory carrying a fixed pairing matrix is the same theory (see below)." Change the summary line in the same way.
- [major] "every pulse's energy divides between the exits, so faint pulses fire both detectors together" (rules-out table, classical wave row). The classical prediction is stated wrongly. With faint classical light and photodetectors, a pulse usually fires neither detector. The classical constraint is statistical: coincidences occur at least at the rate expected by chance from the two single rates (Grangier–Roger–Aspect parameter α ≥ 1). The experiment found α ≈ 0.18, as item (a) correctly says. Fix: "energy divides at every splitter, so the two detectors fire independently: coincidences at least at the chance rate (α ≥ 1); observed α ≈ 0.18."
- [major] "the hands of routes near the classical path line up ... which is why the everyday world shows no fringes" (model 2, Pros). Stationary phase explains why a heavy object follows the classical path. It does not explain the absence of fringes. The path sum still predicts fringes for heavy objects; they are spaced far too finely to resolve, and decoherence by the environment washes them out (molecules of about 2000 atoms have shown fringes). Fix: "for a heavy object only routes very near the classical path add up, which is why it moves classically; fringes are still predicted but are far too finely spaced to see and are washed out by decoherence."
- [major] "The sign change is forced by losslessness" (model 1, beam splitter). This is true only for splitters with real entries. A lossless 50/50 splitter is often written (1/√2)[[1, i],[i, 1]], which has no minus sign. What losslessness forces is a relative phase: a b̄ + c d̄ = 0, so the phase products differ by π. Saying a minus sign is forced will clash with the symmetric splitter the learner will meet, in an answer about why phases are complex. Fix: "Losslessness forces a relative phase between the two outputs. With real entries that phase is a sign change; a symmetric splitter instead multiplies each reflection by i. All choices give the same counts up to a constant shift of φ."
- [major] "real losses lower every count by the same factor" (item (c)). This is false as written. Unequal detector efficiencies scale the two exits differently. Loss in one arm lowers the fringe contrast instead of scaling the counts: with arm-1 transmission t, the sum stays (1+t)/2, but the minimum no longer reaches 0. The opening's "from all to none" is also the ideal case; 1986 had 98% visibility. Fix: "in the ideal lossless case; real losses lower the total, can weight the exits unequally and keep the minimum above zero (98% visibility in 1986)."
- [minor] "a theorem ... for every p from 1 upwards" against "for some power p>0" (opening; model 3). The text concludes that among power rules only p = 2 survives, but the quoted theorem covers only 1 ≤ p < ∞, which leaves 0 < p < 1 unaddressed. The conclusion still holds there. For p < 1, |a+b|^p < |a|^p + |b|^p whenever both a and b are nonzero, so linear maps that preserve the p-total are again only relabellings. Fix: add one sentence saying so, or restrict the claim to p ≥ 1.
- [minor] "the interferometer's family U(φ), φ from 0 to π" (figure a-pnorm-circles). U(φ)(1,0) = ((z+1)/2, (z−1)/2) is a pair of complex numbers, not a point (cos θ, sin θ) on the real circle. The plotted arc shows the sizes (cos(φ/2), sin(φ/2)). Fix: label the arc "sizes of the two exit amplitudes under U(φ), angle φ/2" or "a real rotation by θ, with the same exit chances as U(2θ)". The p-totals annotated in the figure are unaffected.
- [minor] "measured in 'turns' of the extra optical path" (definition of φ). φ = 2π × path/λ is in radians; one turn is 2π. Fix: "measured as an angle: φ = 2π × (extra optical path)/(wavelength), so one wavelength of extra path is one full turn, 2π."
