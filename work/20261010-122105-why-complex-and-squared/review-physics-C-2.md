## Issues
- [major] "three ways for a photon to be an even bet at a polarization sorter" (opening): read literally, this is false. At any one sorter a whole great circle of states is an even bet, not three. Under the reading "even-bet states at H/V", real arrows supply exactly two (D, A) and complex states supply a continuum, not three. The 3-vs-2 count holds only for *mutually even-bet sorters*, which is what the body argues. Fix: "Nature has three polarization sorters that are mutually even bets (each one's sure-bet states are 50/50 at the other two), and an arrow made of one real number per exit allows only two such sorters; that is the shortest..."
- [major] Figure c-sorter-table, R/L row: the forward port of the "cube turned 45°" is labelled "R exit". In the D/A row the identical cube's forward port is the D exit. Model 1 derives $QR = A$ and $QL = D$, so with the text's own convention R leaves by the A port and L by the D port. The figure contradicts the derivation a learner is asked to check. Fix: swap the R and L labels in that row (R sideways, L forward), or add a note: "R leaves by the splitter's A port, since the plate turns R into A."
- [major] Figure c-map: the arrow labels "three sorters ⇒ pairs of reals (one system)" and "parameter count + network test ⇒ pairs of reals", and the footer "Model 3 says why the i and the 2", reintroduce the overstatement that was fixed in the text. The text correctly says these results *exclude one real number per exit* (quaternions are also a consistent pairing, excluded only by other searches), that the pairing is "selected by experiment together with assumptions", and that the three-slit test "shows the world has the square's signature, not why". Fix: relabel to "three sorters ⇒ not one real per exit (one system)", "parameter count + network test ⇒ not real amplitudes with the product rule (pairs)", and change the footer to "Model 3: what consistency fixes (the 2, given Gleason's assumptions) and what experiment selects (the complex pairing)".
- [minor] "the chance is the *squared* length because the square of a sum contains single and pair pieces only" (opening): this presents the pair-only structure as the *reason* for the square. Model 2's cons state correctly that the three-slit test is evidence, not explanation. Fix: "we can tell the chance is the squared length because the square of a sum contains single and pair pieces only, so..."
- [minor] "is, among power rules, the signature of exactly the square": a zero leftover is the square's signature, but a 1% bound only confines the exponent to near 2, since $p = 2+\delta$ gives a leftover of order $\delta$. Fix: add "(powers very close to 2 would give leftovers below the present bound)".
