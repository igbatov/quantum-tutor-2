## Issues
- [major] "weak pulses would fire both detectors together, and they essentially never do" — this gets the classical prediction wrong. A classical wave with realistic (probabilistic, photoelectric) detectors does not fire both detectors on every weak pulse. It predicts coincidences at least as often as chance, i.e. at least (rate 1 x rate 2). Grangier–Roger–Aspect killed the classical wave by measuring far fewer coincidences than that (anticorrelation parameter about 0.2, where classical waves require at least 1). Fix: "A classical wave splits each pulse's energy between the two exits, so with click-producing detectors the two would click together at least as often as chance coincidence predicts. With heralded single photons they click together far less often than that (Grangier, Roger and Aspect, 1986)."
- [major] "neither is a dial that could have been set elsewhere" (also "forced by consistency once states are hands", and the opening "the amplitude must be a pair of real numbers") — this overstates the complex case. Real-amplitude and quaternionic quantum theories are both self-consistent. Consistency fixes the exponent, via Gleason and its assumptions, but not the number field. The number field is selected by experiment: the three-sorter table rules out one real per exit, the network tests rule out real amplitudes with the product rule, and quaternions, which would allow up to five mutually even-bet sorters for a two-level system, are excluded by other evidence and are not addressed here. Also, "it is why the two-slit answer's ... picture scales up without hidden extras" is unsupported. Fix: "Consistency (Gleason) fixes the exponent; the pairing is not forced by consistency, since real and quaternionic versions are consistent theories, but it is selected by experiment (three sorters, network tests) and by extra axioms such as local tomography." Change the opening to "at least a pair of real numbers ... and the pair nature uses combines like complex numbers". Delete the "scales up" clause.
- [major] "so three slits can show no interference beyond what pairs show, which is what is measured" (opening), also "none is seen" — this states as absolute a zero that holds only for ideal, independent slits. The draft's own fine print says real slits give a small nonzero leftover, and that one was measured in 2016. Fix: "...so for ideal slits that each send the same wave whatever the others do, three slits show no interference beyond what pairs show; experiments bound any leftover below about 1% of the two-slit stripes."
- [major] "So with two exits a non-power rule survives everything the sorters measure" — misleading. It survives the 100/0 and 50/50 table only because f(1/sqrt2) = 1/2 exactly. A sorter at an intermediate angle (Malus's law: H prepared, polarizer at 30 degrees) measures f(cos 30 degrees) directly, and measurements of Malus's law bound epsilon tightly. The learner would conclude that two-level experiments can't test the rule beyond powers. Fix: "...so the requirement that two exits total 1 does not fix the rule; only measuring chances at intermediate angles (Malus's law) does, and those measurements bound epsilon to small values. Normalization alone kills it once there are three exits."
- [major] "is also what keeps every quantum calculation finite" — false. A higher-order rule over N alternatives also has finitely many terms, and the infinities quantum calculations do meet (e.g. in field theory) have nothing to do with the Born rule's degree. Fix: delete it, or say "it makes the chance for N alternatives a sum of N single terms and N(N-1)/2 pair terms".
- [major] "the cross terms -i s1 s̄2 + i s̄1 s2 and +i s1 s̄2 - i s̄1 s2" — sign error. With (s1 - i s2)(s̄1 + i s̄2), the cross terms of the first expansion are +i s1 s̄2 - i s̄1 s2, and the second has the opposite signs. They still cancel, but the learner asked for every step to be checkable. Also "pointing at angle arctan(y/x)" is wrong for x < 0 and undefined at x = 0. Fix: swap the two signs, and write "at the angle whose cosine is x/|z| and sine is y/|z|". arg z is acceptable once defined.
- [major] "catching one announces that exactly one partner is on its way" — this is an unnamed idealization. Parametric down-conversion sometimes emits two pairs at once, and the partner can be lost. Fix: "...announces that a partner is almost certainly on its way (occasionally two pairs are emitted together, which experiments keep rare)". Add "single pairs" to the listed idealizations.
- [minor] Figure c-three-slit-patterns, "x ... from -3 to 3": with d = 4w the single-slit envelope's first zero is at x = 4, so the window hides the single-slit side bands, and the "single slits" row looks like one hump. Fix: extend to -6..6, or have the caption say the window shows only the central diffraction band and the single-slit pattern has fainter side bands beyond it.
