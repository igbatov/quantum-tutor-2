## Notes for the learner
- A (Classical-contrast, the atom): suits you if you want a broad first picture of what quantum mechanics is about: fixed energy steps, odds, and why atoms don't collapse. Its weakest point is that interference ("possibilities cancel") gets only one line.
- B (Experiment-first, double slit): suits you if you'd rather deeply understand one experiment and the single rule behind it (add amplitudes, then square). Its weakest point is that atoms and energy levels, where the name "quantum" comes from, get only a brief mention.

## Notes for revision
- A: "tighter wiggles mean more energy of motion" is asserted with no reason or picture. Add one sentence of intuition, or flag that it's being taken on trust. The "Waves of possibility" bullet names the double-slit experiment without saying what it shows. Add a short clause, e.g. "electrons sent one at a time still build up stripes".
- B: The opening sentences define "amplitude" abstractly before I have seen why it's needed. Lead with the plain-language point (possibilities can cancel each other, not just add) and introduce the word after the experiment. "Square the total" should say once in words that this turns the amplitude into a chance.
- C: (not a finalist) Show the double-slit cancellation before relying on it. Cut or shorten the interpretations list for a first-question reader.
