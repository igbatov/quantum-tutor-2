## Issues
- [minor] "a stray photon or air molecule ... does the same" — This is only fully true if the scattered particle carries enough which-slit information. A long-wavelength photon gives only partial information, so the stripes fade without vanishing. Fix: "a stray photon or air molecule that carries away which-slit information does the same; partial information washes the stripes out partially." This also sets up the Check-yourself question.
- [minor] "Standard quantum mechanics simply doesn't say which slit an electron used" — This is accurate for the standard formalism. But the next sentence says interpretations differ only over "what amplitudes ultimately *are*", and some (e.g. pilot-wave) also say the electron did take one definite slit. Fix: "...part ways over what amplitudes are and whether the electron had a definite path, but they all agree on what you'll measure."
- [minor] "their amplitudes no longer meet and add" — This is slightly loose. The amplitudes still exist, but they now lead to distinguishable final situations (different records), so they belong to different outcomes and cannot cancel. The surrounding sentence nearly says this already, and it is fine at conceptual level. Optional fix: "...so they are amplitudes for different final outcomes and can no longer cancel each other."

## Sound points worth keeping
- The +1/−1 and 2² = 4 worked example is correct, including "twice the hits overall, redistributed". The figure spec's P(u) ∝ sinc²(u)·cos²(5πu), with the sinc² envelope for the which-slit case, is the correct far-field form.
- "Observation" is explained as any record-leaving interaction with no role for consciousness. The measurement problem is flagged as open, and decoherence is described as "part of the reason" without overclaiming. Interpretations are handled even-handedly.
