## What is seen (incl. (3) one slit covered)

- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 1 "Quantum Behavior", §1-4 "An experiment with electrons" and §1-5 "The interference of electron waves", [page not verified] — electrons arrive as whole lumps; P12 ≠ P1 + P2, so opening a second hole lowers the count at some points.
- Bach, Pope, Liou & Batelaan, *New J. Phys.* 15, 033018 (2013), [VERIFIED: NJP record via search] — measured one-slit and two-slit patterns and build-up from single detection events.
- Maudlin, *Philosophy of Physics: Quantum Theory*, Princeton UP (2019), ch. 1 "Eight Experiments", pp. 1–35 [VERIFIED: PhilPapers chapter listing via search] — careful statement of what the double-slit data are, kept separate from theory.

## Why a far screen (Fresnel vs Fraunhofer)

- Hecht, *Optics*, 5th ed. (Pearson, 2017), ch. 10 "Diffraction", §10.1 "Preliminary Considerations", pp. 458–465 [VERIFIED: Pearson eLibrary contents via search] — the near-field (Fresnel) vs far-field (Fraunhofer) distinction and the distance criterion.
- Hecht, *Optics*, 5th ed. (2017), ch. 10 "Diffraction", §10.2 "Fraunhofer Diffraction" (incl. §10.2.2 "The Double Slit"), pp. 466–505 for §10.2 [VERIFIED: Pearson eLibrary contents via search; start page of §10.2.2 not verified] — far-screen two-slit pattern as cos² fringes inside the single-slit envelope, incl. missing orders.
- Hecht, *Optics*, 5th ed. (2017), ch. 10 "Diffraction", §10.3 "Fresnel Diffraction", pp. 506–532 [VERIFIED: Pearson eLibrary contents via search] — how the pattern changes shape close to the aperture.

## A which-slit record (incl. partial distinguishability)

- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 1 "Quantum Behavior", §1-6 "Watching the electrons", [page not verified] — watching which hole kills the fringes; dimmer or longer-wavelength light gives partial loss.
- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 3 "Probability Amplitudes", §3-2 "The two-slit interference pattern", [page not verified] — a photon scattered into distinguishable final states: add probabilities; otherwise amplitudes.
- Englert, "Fringe visibility and which-way information: an inequality", *Physical Review Letters* 77, 2154 (1996) [page not verified]; Dürr, Nonn & Rempe, *Nature* 395, 33 (1998) [page not verified]; Buks, Schuster, Heiblum, Mahalu & Umansky, "Dephasing in electron interference by a 'which-path' detector", *Nature* 391, 871 (1998) [page not verified] — partial which-way information partly fades the stripes; experiments with atoms and with electrons in a mesoscopic circuit.

## What this rules out

## Model 1: the wave function

- Griffiths & Schroeter, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*, 3rd ed. (Cambridge UP, 2018), ch. 1 "The Wave Function", §1.1 "The Schrödinger Equation" and §1.2 "The Statistical Interpretation", ch. 1 starts p. 3, §1.2 starts p. 5 [VERIFIED: Cambridge contents/excerpt via search; end page of §1.2 not verified] — the wave function, Schrödinger equation, Born rule, and the realist/orthodox/agnostic positions.
- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 16 "The Dependence of Amplitudes on Position", §16-2 "The wave function" and §16-5 "The Schrödinger equation", [page not verified] — amplitude at every point and its time evolution.
- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 3 "Probability Amplitudes", §3-1 "The laws for combining amplitudes", [page not verified] — add amplitudes first, then square; probability = |total amplitude|².

## Model 2: a sum over paths

- Feynman, *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter*, Princeton UP (1985), ch. 2 "Photons: Particles of Light", "Reflection from a mirror" pp. 38–45 and "Diffraction grating" pp. 45–49 [VERIFIED: D. Styer's synoptic contents of QED (Oberlin) via search] — stopwatch arrows for every path, added tip to tail; arrows near the least-time path line up, others cancel.
- Feynman, "Space-time approach to non-relativistic quantum mechanics", *Reviews of Modern Physics* 20, 367 (1948) [page not verified] — the 1948 path-integral formulation, equivalent to the Schrödinger equation.
- Sakurai & Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*, 3rd ed. (Cambridge UP, 2020), ch. 2 "Quantum Dynamics", §2.6 "Propagators and Feynman Path Integrals", [page not verified; it follows §2.5.2, which starts p. 99 per Cambridge front matter] — equivalence with the Schrödinger picture and the classical limit (stationary action).

## What happens in between? (records, which-way, decoherence)

- Feynman, Leighton & Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. III*, New Millennium ed. (2010), ch. 3 "Probability Amplitudes", §3-2 "The two-slit interference pattern", [page not verified] — amplitudes interfere only for indistinguishable final states; different recorder states mean probabilities add.
- Feynman, *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter*, Princeton UP (1985), ch. 3 "Electrons and Their Interactions", "Measurement" pp. 78–82 [VERIFIED: Styer's synoptic contents via search] — two holes with detectors: different final conditions, so add probabilities instead of arrows.
- Schlosshauer, *Decoherence and the Quantum-to-Classical Transition*, Springer (2007), ch. 2 "The Basic Formalism and Interpretation of Decoherence", starts p. 13 [start page VERIFIED: Springer/Google Books contents via search; section and end page not verified] — which-path entanglement with an environment and loss of interference (overlap of environment states).

## Pilot-wave

- Maudlin, *Philosophy of Physics: Quantum Theory*, Princeton UP (2019), ch. 5 "Pilot Wave Theories", pp. 137–172 [VERIFIED: PhilPapers chapter listing via search] — definite positions guided by the wave; nonlocality; the difficulty of extending it to field theory.
- Bell, *Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics*, 2nd ed. - Philippidis, Dewdney & Hiley, "Quantum interference and the quantum potential", *Il Nuovo Cimento B* 52, 15 (1979) [page not verified] — the computed two-slit trajectories that do not cross the symmetry line and bunch in bright fringes.

## Objective collapse

- Bell, *Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics*, 2nd ed. (2004), ch. 22 "Are there quantum jumps?", pp. 201–212 [VERIFIED (secondary): page range from a citing paper via search; consistent with ch. 23 starting p. 213 per PhilPapers] — the GRW spontaneous-localization model: rare per particle, fast for many particles.
- Maudlin, *Philosophy of Physics: Quantum Theory*, Princeton UP (2019), ch. 4 "Collapse Theories and the Problem of Local Beables", pp. 94–136 [VERIFIED: PhilPapers chapter listing via search] — GRW as a modified theory with new constants.
