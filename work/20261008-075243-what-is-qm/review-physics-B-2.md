## Issues
- [major] "only two possible answers, ever: two spots, never a third" — this absolute is false for silver itself. The silver nucleus is also slightly magnetic (I = 1/2), and in a weak enough field the atom's hyperfine levels separate differently: two of them have almost no push at first, so they land near the centre and give a third, undeflected beam. Rabi-style weak-field deflection experiments used exactly this effect to count beams and work out nuclear spins. Two spots is the strong-field result, which is the regime Stern and Gerlach worked in. Fix: "for a silver atom in a strong magnet like Stern and Gerlach's, that question has two possible answers: two spots, no third (in very weak fields the nucleus's own faint magnetism can split things further; other kinds of atom give other numbers of spots)."
- [minor] "*Seen:* all come out 'up'" / "*Seen:* half left, half right" — these are labelled "Seen", but the paragraph's last sentence says this clean three-magnet sequence is the textbook idealization, not a performed experiment. The hedge is there, so nothing is false, but the label and the hedge pull against each other. Fix: label the three results "*Expected (ideal set-up), and seen in equivalent experiments:*", or move the "realized in several forms … textbook idealization" sentence to the start of the paragraph.
- [minor] "is still an added rule, so both models share that open question" — two paragraphs later, many-worlds is given "Pro: no added rule", so this reads as if it were settled. Fix: "in the standard formulation, the last step … is an added rule; whether it must be is one of the open questions below."
- [minor] "for several atoms the guidance acts instantly across any distance" — in pilot-wave theory, one atom's motion depends on a distant atom's position only when their wave is entangled (does not factor). Fix: "for several entangled atoms the guidance acts instantly across any distance (though it cannot be used to send signals)."
- [minor] "The same globe describes anything with two possible answers" — the globe fits a system whose every question has two answers (a two-level system). It does not fit any two-answer question asked of a larger system, such as "left half or right half of the box?". Fix: "describes any system whose questions all have two possible answers."
