## Issues
- [major] "He then found how to multiply such tables and a rule they must obey, which pins down … the same ladder" — This credits Heisenberg with getting hydrogen's ladder, which is wrong history. His July 1925 paper worked out a toy oscillator, not hydrogen. The tables were recognised as matrices, and the rule pq − qp = iħ was written in full, by Born and Jordan (with Heisenberg, 1925). Hydrogen's ladder came from this method only in January 1926, in Pauli's calculation. The "why this model" history needs to be right. Fix: "Heisenberg (1925), with Born and Jordan, found how to multiply such tables and a rule they must obey; early in 1926 Pauli used it to work out hydrogen's rungs: the same ladder."
- [minor] "for the fitting patterns described next it can, at any one moment, be taken as simply positive or negative" — Read as "every fitting pattern is real at each moment", this is not true. Hydrogen's rungs above the bottom one hold several patterns each, and some natural ones (those carrying orbital angular momentum, e^{imφ}) have clock hands that point in different directions from place to place. What is true is that a full set of fitting patterns *can be chosen* to be real. Fix: "(the fitting patterns can be chosen so that, at any one moment, the number is simply positive or negative)".
- [minor] "Franck–Hertz is the same ladder seen without light" — Just before this the ladder is hydrogen's, but Franck–Hertz measures mercury's. Fix: "Franck–Hertz shows the same kind of ladder, mercury's, without light".
- [minor] "it is the main tool of particle physics" — In particle physics the sum runs over field configurations, not over "every route the electron could take", and the everyday working tool, Feynman diagrams, can be derived from either formulation. Fix: "in its extension to fields it is a central tool of particle physics; it is famously awkward for atoms."
- [minor] "occasionally bounce straight back" — Roughly 1 in 8000 alphas was deflected by more than 90°, and very few came back near 180°. Fix: "occasionally bounce back".
