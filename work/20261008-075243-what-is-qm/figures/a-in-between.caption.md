Why a record stops the cancelling (schematic, relative units). Both panels look at the same spot on the screen, one that is a dark stripe when nothing records the slit: all the ways through the left slit, taken together, bring amplitude +1, and all the ways through the right slit, taken together, bring −1 (drawn as clock hands pointing up and down). Left, no record: both slits' contributions end in the same final situation, "dot here", so their amplitudes are added first, (+1) + (−1) = 0, and the chance there is 0. Right, a perfectly readable record: the two slits' contributions end in two different final situations, "dot here, recorder says left" and "dot here, recorder says right"; amplitudes in different final situations are never added, so each slit gives its own chance and the chance at the spot is 1 + 1 = 2, the two one-slit chances added. Notice: the cancelling is lost because the final situations differ, whether or not anyone reads the recorder. Idealization: a recorder whose two states can be told apart perfectly; if they can be told apart only partly, the two slits' amplitudes partly cancel and fainter stripes survive.
