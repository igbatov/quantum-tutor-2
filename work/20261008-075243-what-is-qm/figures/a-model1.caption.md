Computed amplitudes and chances across the middle of a far screen, for the same ideal set-up as the earlier figures (only ±2.6 stripe spacings shown). Top: the amplitude arriving from each slit and their sum. Amplitudes are really clock hands (complex numbers), so this panel is a simplification: it shows only the part of each hand along the left-slit hand's direction (the real part, with all angles measured from the left-slit hand). Notice the bright spot in the middle, where the two amplitudes are alike (1 + 1 = 2), and the dark spot half a stripe out, where they are opposite (+0.97 and −0.97, close to +1 and −1) and the sum is 0. Middle: the full clock hands at five spots. Between bright and dark the right-slit hand points partly sideways, which the top panel does not show. Bottom: the chance is the squared size of the full sum (bold): 4 at the bright spot and 0 at the dark spot. Adding the two chances instead (dashed) gives about 2 at both spots and no stripes. Between bright and dark spots the chance is not the square of the top panel's bold curve, because that curve leaves out the sideways part.
