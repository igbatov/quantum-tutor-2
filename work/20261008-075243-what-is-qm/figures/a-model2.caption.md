Left (schematic, not to scale): a few of the routes from the source through each slit to one spot on the screen. Right (computed): the clock hands for those routes, added tip to tail. The geometry is the ideal set-up of the earlier figures, with source and screen each 100,000 wavelengths from the wall. Each slit is cut into 8 strips. Each thin hand stands for the routes through one strip, and its angle is set by the length of the route through the strip's centre. All hands are drawn the same length; the real lengths differ by under 0.1%. The bold arrows, drawn just below, are each slit's group total. The whole diagram is turned so that the left-slit total points right; only the angles between hands matter. Bright spot, one stripe spacing out: within each slit the hands fan out, so each group total is a bit shorter than its 8 hands laid end to end, but the two totals point the same way. The total is 1.8 and the chance 3.3 (4 at the centre). Dark spot, half a stripe spacing out: the right-slit group total points straight back along the left one (its hands trace the mirror-image arc), so the total is 0 and so is the chance. With 400 strips per slit instead of 8 the results are the same.
