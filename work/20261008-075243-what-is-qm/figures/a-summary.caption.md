The closing summary in four steps (schematic layout; the dots and curves are computed for the same ideal far-screen two-slit set-up as the earlier figures). 1: each electron arrives as one whole dot. 2: only the chances can be predicted; 4,000 dots, drawn at random from the computed chances, build up the stripes, but no single dot's place can be predicted. 3: at each spot the amplitudes from the two slits add before squaring; alike (+1 and +1) gives 2 and chance 4, opposite (+1 and −1) gives 0 and chance 0, where adding chances would give 2 at both spots. 4: with a perfect which-slit record (idealized), the fine stripes vanish and the pattern becomes the two one-slit patterns added (dashed: a wide band with faint side bands), with the same total number of electrons as the striped curve. Notice that step 4 removes the zeros of step 3, so the dark stripes fill in. Bottom: the same amplitude rule, plus the rule for identical particles, underlies atoms, chemical bonds, transistors and lasers.
