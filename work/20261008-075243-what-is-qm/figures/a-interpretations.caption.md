The four views of what one electron does between slits and dot. Three panels are schematic: Copenhagen-style (the wave function is a tool that gives the chances; the result is one dot; it gives no answer to "which slit?"), many-worlds (electron and screen become one joint state with a branch for each place the dot could be, drawn here as five branches although there are many more; we see the one dot in our branch), and objective collapse (the ripple stays spread out in flight and localizes at random almost at once only when the arrival sets huge numbers of particles in the screen moving). The pilot-wave panel (bottom left) is computed: paths of the electron steered by the wave, for 36 equally likely starting points, with time standing in for distance from the slits. Idealizations: one electron speed, motion across the beam only, and slit openings with soft (Gaussian) edges, which is why the pattern on the right has no faint side bands. Notice: every path goes through one slit and none crosses the dashed centre line; paths bend away from the dark lines and bunch into the bright stripes, so the paths together reproduce the usual pattern of chances (checked with 3,000 paths).
