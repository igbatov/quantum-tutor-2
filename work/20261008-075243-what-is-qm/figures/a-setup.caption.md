The two-slit set-up seen from the side (schematic, not to scale). Electrons leave the source one at a time, meet a wall with two narrow slits of width a whose centres are a distance d apart (drawn, as in the other figures, with d = 4a), and each one that gets through makes a single dot on a screen far away; the break in the distance arrow means the real screen is much further off than drawn, and on a far screen the stripes are far wider than the slit separation, so the screen is not drawn to the same scale as the slits. The 14 dots are a real random sample from the ideal far-screen pattern of the later figures, restricted to the part of the screen drawn. Notice: each electron leaves one whole dot, and a handful of dots shows no clear pattern yet; stripes become clear after about a thousand (next figure).
