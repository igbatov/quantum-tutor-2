## Issues
- [minor] "the same rule applied to hydrogen predicts the colours it emits" — Grammatically, "the same rule" points back to "wavelength set by the electron's speed". Applied to hydrogen, that is the de Broglie standing-wave-on-an-orbit picture. A bound electron has no single speed, and that picture would have to be unlearned. The intended meaning (the Schrödinger equation) is right. Fix: "and the same equation, applied to the electron bound in a hydrogen atom, predicts the colours hydrogen emits."
- [minor] "supporters reply that this is just the ripple equation taken seriously" — This answers a different objection (that many worlds is extravagant), not the one about what the chances mean. As written, the supporters' side of the debate looks emptier than it is. Fix: "supporters reply that the chances follow from how a rational observer should bet across branches, an argument critics still dispute."
- [minor] "a small clock hand whose angle is set by the route's length" — In Feynman's 1948 formulation, the angle is set by the route's action. It comes down to "length" only for a free electron of fixed energy, and even then only as the fixed-energy version of the method. The condition is partly named ("electrons all at one speed"), and the double-slit conclusion is unaffected. Fix: "...set by the route's length (true here, for free electrons all at one speed; in general by a quantity called the action)".
- [minor] "a physical trace of which slit each electron used" — This presupposes that each electron used one slit. The text itself later says "'which slit?' has no answer the theory gives" (Copenhagen-style). Fix: "a physical trace that would distinguish passage through the left slit from passage through the right".

## Sound points worth keeping
- All round-one fixes are in and correct. These include: the wave's reality is left to the interpretations; partial vs. perfect distinguishability is handled consistently in the record section; objective collapse is treated as a modified theory, with the heating/radiation searches named; and the dust grain is given two reasons, wavelength and decoherence. The figure matches the text: P1 = sinc²(X/4), both slits 4·P1·cos²(πX), zeros at ±0.5 and ±1.5, the missing bright stripe at X = 4, and side bands shown in the inset.
- The classical predictions are stated with their conditions. The single-slit pattern is described correctly, with its faint side bands. The interpretations are labelled as open, and the path-integral formulation is correctly called equivalent to the wave function, with "takes every path" flagged as a slogan.
