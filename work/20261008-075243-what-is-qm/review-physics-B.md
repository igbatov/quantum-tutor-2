## Issues
- [major] "every view below gives identical counts for everything above" (also "The four interpretations agree on every count") — this is false for objective collapse. It is a rival theory, not an interpretation: it changes the equations and predicts deviations (loss of interference, slight heating). The deviations are far too small to see with single silver atoms, but they are not zero. The draft itself calls it "testable" two paragraphs later, which contradicts this claim. Fix: "Copenhagen-style, many-worlds and pilot-wave give identical counts for everything above; objective collapse modifies the theory slightly, and its predicted differences are far too small to show up with single atoms." In "How they relate", separate the three equivalent interpretations from the one rival theory, which is testable in principle.
- [major] "questions with only a few possible answers" (Zoomed-out summary) — this is false as a description of quantum mechanics in general. Position and momentum have a continuum of possible answers, and a hydrogen atom has infinitely many energy levels. The opening line hedges correctly ("some questions"), but the summary drops that hedge. Fix: "some questions, like this one, have only a few possible answers (here just two), where classical physics expected a continuous range."
- [major] "that chance rule has been checked ... with the polarization of light" — the rule is stated in terms of the angle on the globe, and the only worked example (a magnet turned by 90° gives 50/50) equates the globe angle with the angle the apparatus is turned. For light, the globe angle is twice the polarizer angle. Crossed polarizers (90°) give 0, not 50/50, and 45° gives 50/50. As written, a learner will carry "turn by 90°, get 50/50" over to polarizers. Fix: add "(for light, the angle on the globe is twice the angle the polarizer is turned by, so crossed polarizers are opposite poles and 45° gives 50/50)", or don't mention light here.
- [minor] "answers that update the state and erase others" — this holds only for incompatible questions. Asking Z again erases nothing, as the draft's own row 1 shows. Fix: "erase the answers to incompatible questions", which matches the opening line's "can erase".
- [minor] "each outcome is determined but unpredictable in practice" — in pilot-wave theory the unpredictability is in principle. Once positions follow the |wave|² distribution, no measurement can learn an atom's starting position more precisely than that. The outcome also depends on how the magnet is set, not only on the starting position. Fix: "determined by where in the beam it started and by how the magnet is set, but unpredictable even in principle, because no measurement can learn that starting point more finely than the wave allows."
- [minor] "Quantum mechanics is the physics of atoms, electrons and light" — this reads as if quantum mechanics is limited to those systems. It applies to everything; its effects are just most visible at small scales. Fix: "Quantum mechanics is our basic theory of matter and light; its effects are most visible with atoms, electrons and light."
- [minor] "hence two spots, never a third" — this is true for silver, but atoms whose magnetism comes from more than one unpaired electron, or from orbital motion, give three or more spots. Fix: "for silver, two spots, never a third (other atoms can give more, always a countable few)."

## Sound points worth keeping
- The Stern–Gerlach description is careful: the real 1922 plate (bands joined at their ends) is kept separate from the narrow-beam idealization, the classical isotropic prediction (a uniform continuous band) is correct, and it says plainly that spin is not rotation.
- The fixed-label sieve picture is killed cleanly by Z-X-Z. The recombination point shows that a magnet that only scrambles labels can't explain the data. Model 1's chance rule is right (certain at the pole, 50/50 at 90° on the globe), and the "as far as any experiment can tell" hedge on the oven's random spread is correct.
