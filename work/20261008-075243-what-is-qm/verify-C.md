## Notes
- Item 1 passes because, with UV defined as below 400 nm (ISO 21348), only four Balmer lines are visible. If the figure keeps the 380-700 nm window, the text should mention the faint edge lines or the figure should narrow to 400-700 nm, so that text and figure agree.
- "almost always as a single photon" is fine; the notable exception is the metastable 2s state (two-photon decay), the same state as in item 12.
- Not computable, left to the critic. The text says "objective collapse adds effects too small to have shown up in any atomic experiment so far". That holds for GRW/CSL at standard parameters, but the parameter-free Diosi-Penrose model was excluded in 2020 by a spontaneous X-ray emission experiment on germanium (Donadi et al.). A safer wording is "effects too small to have shown up in atomic spectra".
- Not computable: the claim that 4.9 eV is the level "electrons most readily lift it to". The lowest excited Hg level is actually 6s6p 3P0 at 4.67 eV, and modern analyses find that 3P0 and 3P2 also contribute to the Franck-Hertz spacing. The text says "a rung 4.9 eV above its lowest", not "the lowest rung", so it is not wrong. The parenthetical is a simplification.
- The quoted wavelengths are air values. Vacuum values are 656.47, 486.27, 434.17 and 410.29 nm, which round to the same figures.
