## Issues
- [major] "the route through the left slit brings amplitude +1" (a-in-between caption; in-figure "the two routes meet and can cancel"; a-summary step 4 "A record of the route") — this counts one route per slit, but a slit has width, so there are infinitely many ways through it. a-model2, two figures earlier, draws many routes per slit, so these figures contradict it. Fix: caption: "all the ways through the left slit, taken together, bring amplitude +1; all the ways through the right slit, together, bring −1". In-figure: "the two slits' combined amplitudes meet and can cancel". Summary title: "4. A record of which slit stops the cancelling".
- [major] "One number decides which case you are in" (Why a far screen) — this holds only for one slit shape (here, separation four times the width). Whether the bands stay separate depends on how much each band spreads, which is set by the slit width. With very narrow slits (a ≪ d) the bands already overlap and form strong stripes while d²/(λL) is still much larger than 1. The "about 90%" figure is also just the value for the middle panel (N = 3), not a general one. Fix: "For slits like these (separation four times the width), one number decides…; for other slit shapes the slit width matters too." Also: "at a middle distance (the middle panel), opening the second slit cuts the hits at the darkest stripe by about 90%".
- [major] "no spot where opening the second slit clearly takes hits away" (Why a far screen, close case) — the top panel of a-three-distances contradicts this. In the dim gap, the minima of the faint stripes fall below the left-slit-only curve, and some ripple dips on the bands do too. The effect is small in absolute terms but not absent, and at some gap spots it removes a large share of the local hits. Fix: "opening the second slit takes hits away only at a few dim spots in the gap and on the ripples, a loss of a few per cent of the peak brightness."
- [major] "they agree on every prediction: no experiment has ever told them apart" (a-how-relate figure and caption) — for pilot-wave, this holds only under its standard assumption that starting positions follow the usual chances (quantum equilibrium). The text itself says pilot-wave's extension to light and high-energy physics is "less settled", so "every prediction" as written goes beyond the text. Also, pilot-wave adds an equation (the steering rule) and particle positions; whether that makes it a "reading" of the theory or a separate theory with the same predictions is itself debated. Fix: "they agree on every prediction for experiments like this one (pilot-wave given its usual assumption about starting positions); no experiment has told them apart". Optionally add "(whether pilot-wave is a reading or a separate theory with the same predictions is debated)".
- [minor] "the right-slit group comes straight back along the left one" (a-model2 caption) — the picture shows the right-slit hands returning along the mirror-image arc, not along the left arc. Only the group total comes straight back. Fix: "the right-slit group total points straight back along the left one (its hands trace the mirror-image arc)".
- [minor] "the stripes appear only after thousands (next figure)" (a-setup caption) — the 1,000-electron panel of the next figure already shows clear stripes. Fix: "stripes become clear after about a thousand (next figure)".
- [minor] "same position in stripe spacings (range of figure 2)" (a-three-distances, top axis) — the ±8 range belongs to a-one-vs-both, which is the third figure in the document; the second is the dots build-up. Fix: "(range of the one-slit vs both-slits figure)".
- [minor] "the same amplitude rule, plus one rule for identical particles … underlies atoms" (a-summary bottom box and caption) — this leaves out the electric forces, which are also needed. Fix: "the same amplitude rule, applied to electrons and nuclei held by electric forces, plus one rule for identical particles…".
