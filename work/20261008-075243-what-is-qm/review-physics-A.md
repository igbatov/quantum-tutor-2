## Issues
- [major] "Nor is it a wave of any stuff" — This takes a side on interpretation and states it as fact. It also contradicts the text's own later sections: "Many-worlds. The wave function is everything" and "Objective collapse. The ripple is real". What is established is only that you never detect part of an electron. Fix: "And unlike a water wave, it is not an electron smeared out that you could catch in pieces: you never detect part of an electron. Whether the wave is itself something real is one of the questions the interpretations below answer differently."
- [major] "parts tied to different recorder states can't cancel; their chances just add" — This holds only when the recorder states can be told apart perfectly. If they are different but only partly distinguishable, the parts still partly cancel. That is the "partly fades the stripes" case the text itself describes earlier, so as written the sentence contradicts it. Fix: "parts tied to recorder states that can be told apart perfectly can't cancel, and their chances just add; if the states are only partly distinguishable, the parts partly cancel and the stripes partly survive."
- [major] "with identical predictions for everything above" / "and only for large objects" — Objective collapse is a modified theory, not just an interpretation. Its predictions are not identical even for one electron; the departures are just far too small to see. Those departures are also not confined to large objects: collapse models predict slight spontaneous heating and faint X-ray emission from ordinary matter. Underground detector searches for that X-ray emission ruled out the simplest Diósi–Penrose version (around 2020), and limits also come from cantilevers and gravitational-wave instruments, not only from large molecules. Fix: "The first three are interpretations with identical predictions. Objective-collapse theories change the equation slightly. For one electron the change is far too small to see, but it grows with the amount of matter, and experiments (large-molecule interference, heating and faint-radiation searches) have narrowed its constants without finding it." Change the "How they relate" sentence and the Con line to match.
- [major] "a dust grain shows no stripes in practice because air and light record its position" — Decoherence is given as the only reason, which overstates it. At any ordinary speed a dust grain's wavelength is so tiny that its stripes would be far too fine to see even in a dark vacuum. A learner using "faster/heavier → narrower stripes" from earlier would be misled. Fix: "a dust grain shows no stripes in practice: its wavelength is so tiny that the stripes would be far too fine to resolve, and in any case air and light constantly record its position, which washes out the cancelling (physicists call this decoherence)."
- [minor] "Electrons are sent so slowly that each has landed before the next leaves" — "Slowly" reads as low speed. The experiments used fast electrons (Tonomura: 50 kV) at a low *rate*. Also, the electrons are emitted at random times, so "each" holds only on average. Fix: "Electrons are sent so rarely that each has almost always landed before the next one leaves."
- [minor] "a screen far away compared with the slit spacing" — The far-field condition, which gives the stated cos² × side-band pattern, is stronger than "far compared with the slit spacing". The distance must be large compared with (slit size)²/wavelength. Fix: "a screen far enough away that the pattern no longer changes shape with distance".
- [minor] "One idea survives, a particle steered by a wave" — Read literally, this says the pilot-wave model is the only picture left. But Model 1 survives too, and so do other proposals. Fix: "One picture in which the electron does have a definite path survives: a particle steered by a wave, the pilot-wave model below."
- [minor] "the physics of matter and light at the scale of atoms and below" — This suggests the theory stops applying above atomic scale. Superconductors, lasers, neutron stars and the text's own dust grain show otherwise. Fix: "the physics of matter and light; its effects are most obvious at the scale of atoms and below".

## Sound points worth keeping
- The classical expectations are stated correctly and with their conditions (straight vs. bouncing balls; "a second slit could only add hits"). The single-slit pattern is described correctly, with its faint side bands. The figure numbers check out: P1 ≈ 0.95 at |X| = 0.5, ≈ 0.62 at |X| = 1.5, and the side-band peak is ≈ 0.047 near |X| ≈ 5.7.
- The experimental history is accurate: Merli–Missiroli–Pozzi 1976 and Tonomura 1989 with biprisms, Batelaan's group in 2013 with real slits, and which-path tests with atoms, photons and a mesoscopic electron circuit. The path-integral section is correctly described as equivalent to the wave function, and "takes every path" is correctly flagged as a slogan.
