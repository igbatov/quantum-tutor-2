## Issues
- [major] "The same pattern appears in light from the Sun" — Sunlight is a continuous rainbow crossed by *dark* hydrogen lines (absorption, Fraunhofer C and F), not hydrogen's bright-line pattern. Many galaxies also show hydrogen as dark lines. Coming right after "filament gives a rainbow, hydrogen gives lines", this tells the learner the Sun's light looks like the tube's, which is false. Fix: "The same wavelengths show up in sunlight as dark gaps in its rainbow (hydrogen in the Sun's outer layers absorbing exactly those colours), and as bright or dark lines in light from galaxies billions of light-years away, every line shifted by the same fraction…"
- [major] "a number at each point of space that can be positive or negative" — In general an amplitude is a complex number, with a size and a phase. Only the fitting (stationary) patterns can be chosen real, and even those carry a phase that turns in time. The skill insists on "amplitudes (complex numbers)". "Positive or negative" is a lie-to-children the learner will have to unlearn. Also, the squared size is a chance *per bit of volume*, not "the chance … there". Fix: "a number at each point of space, with a size and a direction like a clock hand (for the fitting patterns it can be taken as simply positive or negative); the squared size tells how likely you are to find the whole electron in a small region there if you look."
- [major] Figure c-ladder-lines, strip over "380-700 nm" with only "four thin bright lines" — Hydrogen's series continues inside that window: lines at 397, 389 and 384 nm (fainter, at the violet edge) and on towards 365 nm. A strip labelled 380–700 nm that shows exactly four lines hides real features. Fix: plot 400–700 nm, or add the fainter lines at 397/389/384 nm with the label "fainter lines crowding towards the ultraviolet". In the text, say "four bright visible lines".
- [major] "matter comes in allowed states, which form ladders" — This is the summary of what QM is about, and as an absolute it is false. Only bound systems (atoms, molecules) have separate rungs. A free electron, or an electron pulled off an atom, can have any energy, and crystals have nearly continuous bands, not ladders (the later "ladders of … crystals" repeats the error). Fix: "matter that is held together, like an atom or a molecule, has only certain allowed energies, a ladder (a free particle can have any energy; in crystals the rungs merge into bands)", and "the levels of atoms and the bands of crystals are what lasers, LEDs and transistors are built on."
- [major] "fails for helium and every heavier atom, has no rule for line brightness" — This overstates the history. Bohr's model works for every one-electron ion: its 1913 success was explaining the ionized-helium (He⁺) lines that had been credited to hydrogen. Via the correspondence principle (Bohr, Kramers 1919) it also gave rough estimates of line brightness. Fix: "fails for neutral helium and every atom with more than one electron, gives only rough estimates of line brightness".
- [minor] "can give an atom a whole rung-gap or nothing" (also "not bit by bit") — An electron bouncing off an atom (an elastic collision) gives up a tiny amount of energy as recoil, and above the ionization energy any amount can be transferred. Fix: "can change the atom's inner energy only by a whole rung-gap (apart from a tiny recoil when it bounces off)".
- [minor] "The analogy ends there: nothing is vibrating" — The amplitude's phase does turn in time, at a rate set by the energy. What is absent is any material vibration, and the caption already says so correctly. Fix: "nothing material is vibrating".
- [minor] Check yourself: "how many different colours of light could it give off" — The intended answer is three (3→2, 3→1, 2→1), but that is an upper limit. Some drops can be very rare (selection rules), some may be invisible, and two gaps could be equal. Fix: "at most how many different wavelengths of light…".

## Sound points worth keeping
- The Franck–Hertz description names its idealizations ("shifted a volt or so", "drops are not to zero", and the parenthetical about mercury's 4.9 eV rung). The classical collapse time (~10⁻¹¹ s), the hydrogen energies and wavelengths, and the pilot-wave account of the ground state are all correct.
- The interpretations section keeps observation separate from interpretation, notes that all four give the same lines, and correctly marks objective collapse as the only one predicting a difference. It also states the Born rule as an added postulate.
