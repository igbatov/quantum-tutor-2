## Issues
- [major] "Setting $c=0$ is the *choice* of which frame is called 'at rest'". This is false as written. Step 5 found that the law $\omega(k)=\hbar k^2/2m+ck+\omega_0$ is boost-covariant with the *same* $c$ in every frame, so no boost removes the $ck$ term. In every frame the minimum of $\omega$ sits at $k=-mc/\hbar$, so "the frame where $\omega$ is smallest at $k=0$" does not exist. The "seen from a frame moving at $-c$" reading is wrong for the same reason. Fix: "$c$ is fixed by the classical limit, the same assumption as $p'=p-mv$: the group velocity $d\omega/dk=\hbar k/m+c$ must equal $p/m$, so $c=0$. Equivalently, the $ck$ term can be removed by the rephasing $\psi\to e^{imcx/\hbar}\psi$, which changes which operator is called momentum. It is a convention about $p$, not a frame choice." Change the ledger row to match. Also state that Step 5 assumes $\omega(k)$ is differentiable.
- [major] "I *assume* $U$ differentiable" / ledger "continuity ⇒ differentiable". For an unbounded $H$, and the $H_\text{free}$ found in §4 is unbounded, $U(t)$ is *not* differentiable as an operator (in norm). Only $U(t)|\psi\rangle$ is differentiable, and only for $|\psi\rangle$ in the dense domain of $H$. So the heuristic, as stated, contradicts the result. Fix: "assume $U(t)|\psi\rangle$ is differentiable in $t$ for every $|\psi\rangle$ in a dense set (Stone's theorem guarantees exactly this). Read $dU/dt=AU$ as acting on such states." In finite dimensions, continuity ⇒ differentiability is itself a theorem; say so.
- [major] "Each electron arrives as one click at one place" (listed under Davisson–Germer). Davisson and Germer did not observe this: they measured current with a Faraday collector. Single-electron build-up of an interference pattern came much later (Merli, Missiroli & Pozzi 1974; Tonomura et al., Am. J. Phys. 57, 117 (1989)). §2's "ruled out by single clicks" depends on this, so it needs its own experiment and source. Fix: move the sentence to a separate item citing Tonomura 1989.
- [major] "the 1975 data agreed with it to about 1% after correcting for the crystal's bending". This looks overstated. From memory, which should be checked against the paper: the 1975 result agreed within roughly 10%. The ~1% level came with later versions (Staudenmann et al., PRA 21, 1419 (1980); Littrell et al., PRA 56, 1767 (1997)), and those found a small (~1%) discrepancy that persisted. Fix: "the 1975 data agreed with it within the experiment's uncertainty, roughly 10%; later, more precise runs reached ~1% and found a small residual discrepancy still discussed."
- [major] "boost = phase × relabelling | theorem (lemma) given (G1) + (L)". (L) is linearity of *time evolution*. The lemma needs the *boost map* to be linear, which is a separate assumption. Alternatively it follows from Wigner's theorem plus continuity in $v$ (boosts connect to the identity, which excludes the antiunitary option). Fix: add "(G0) the boost acts by a linear map: *assumption* (or Wigner + continuity)" and cite it in the lemma and the ledger. A smaller point: in the lemma sketch, the support argument already follows from $|T\psi_1|=|\psi_1|$ alone. The $\psi_1+\psi_2$ remark is what shows locality.
- [major] "No classical wave has a mass in its phase; this one does." This is false as an absolute. The classical Klein–Gordon field has a mass parameter in its dispersion. The caption's "a massless classical wave would give a tilt-independent intensity" is also not exact: in the real apparatus, tilting bends the crystal (the correction you cite) and that shifts the phase, and light gets a tiny gravitational phase as well. Fix: "A classical wave like sound or light, with $\lambda=h/p$ put in by hand, has no particle mass $m$ in its phase, so it cannot give a shift $\propto m^2$." Caption: "…would give no gravity-induced fringe shift (apart from apparatus effects such as crystal bending)."
- [minor] Sources. Ballentine §3.1–3.2 are Wigner's theorem and space-time symmetries. The equation of motion from time displacement is §3.7. The Galilei transformation of the Schrödinger wavefunction is §4.3 (worth citing for §4 alongside §3.3–3.4). Ballentine §2.1 does not discuss classical candidates, so drop it from §2. Sakurai & Napolitano §2.4 postulates $H=p^2/2m+V$ and does not derive it from boosts, so cite it as "postulated there". I cannot confirm the Weinberg *Lectures* section numbers (§3.5 for Stone, §3.6 for Galilei); Weinberg also uses $U=e^{-iHt}$ without proving Stone's theorem. Cite "ch. 3" unless checked. I cannot confirm Rauch & Werner ch. 7 either; cite "the chapter on gravitational and inertial effects". The journal citations (DG Phys. Rev. 30, 705; COW PRL 34, 1472; WSC PRL 42, 1103; Bargmann Ann. Math. 59, 1) look correct. No page numbers are given, which is good.
- [minor] "the experimental face of 'the phase of a matter wave depends on the frame'". The rotating-frame (Sagnac) effect concerns a non-inertial frame. It is related to boost covariance but is not the same thing, so say so. "have not been tested directly" is an absolute I cannot confirm; soften to "are not, to my knowledge, tested directly". Also in §5: objective-collapse models modify both (L) and (U) (their evolution is nonlinear and stochastic), not (U) alone. In (U), norm preservation gives $U^\dagger U=1$; the other half, $UU^\dagger=1$, needs invertibility, which comes from (C) with negative $t$.
