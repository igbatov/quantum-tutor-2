## Issues
- [major] "Observed: the flip fraction is a smooth function of $t$" — this is not what Rabi et al. (Phys. Rev. 53, 318, 1938) measured. They swept the static field at fixed rf frequency, so the transit time was set by the beam velocity, and they saw a resonance dip in beam intensity, averaged over the velocity distribution. Flip probability as a function of pulse length (Rabi oscillation in time) was first seen later, e.g. Torrey 1949 (transient nutation in NMR); today it is routine with trapped ions and atoms. Fix: "Rabi (1938) found sharp, reproducible resonances. Later time-domain experiments (Torrey 1949; trapped ions today) vary the pulse length $t$ and see a flip fraction that changes smoothly with $t$, close to $\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$ on resonance."
- [major] "COW [heuristic]: … time-dependent boost … gives the observed $Mg\Delta z\,T/\hbar$, with the same $M$" — three hidden assumptions. (a) Going to a freely falling frame needs the equivalence principle (gravitational mass = inertial mass), and the Galilei group does not supply it. (b) A uniformly accelerated frame change is not a Galilei transformation, and the G2 phase $Mav/\hbar$ (constant translation and boost) is not the phase this calculation needs. (c) With $T$ the transit time, the $M$ in $Mg\Delta z\,T/\hbar$ is the gravitational mass, because the inertial mass sits inside $T$. Fix: "In the lab, a potential $V=Mgz$ (an assumption, §G6) gives the phase $Mg\Delta z\,T/\hbar$. Equating this $M$ with the boost central charge is the equivalence principle, an extra assumption that COW tests." The §1 line "the phase of ψ carries a mass" should also point to this.
- [major] "All mainstream interpretations (… objective collapse) use the same $U(t)$" — false as written. GRW/CSL have no "between measurements": their spontaneous collapses happen all the time, and they change the dynamics itself. They also break A1, not only A2: the state-vector evolution is nonlinear and stochastic, and only the averaged density-matrix evolution is linear. Fix: "Copenhagen-style, many-worlds and pilot-wave all use this $U(t)$ (pilot-wave and many-worlds at all times). Objective-collapse models are different theories: they add a stochastic, nonlinear term that breaks A1 and A2 by a tiny amount, which experiments are testing."
- [minor] Rabi figure "two back-to-back resonant pulses … same drive phase" — in the lab frame an oscillating drive makes $H$ depend on $t$, so A3 in the form $U(s)U(t)=U(s+t)$ fails there. It holds in the rotating frame within the rotating-wave approximation, and only if the drive is phase-continuous. Also, $\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$ is exact only on resonance in the RWA, and the plotted $P(t)$ shows a probability, not the state. Fix the caption: "…phase-continuous drive, rotating frame (RWA); the flip probability after the two pulses equals the single-pulse value at 80 µs".
- [minor] "unless $\alpha(\psi_1)=\alpha(\psi_2)$ the vectors … would be dependent" — true only for linearly independent $\psi_1,\psi_2$, which the text doesn't say. The step "$\varphi=\lambda av+\dots$" also assumes $\varphi$ is differentiable (smooth), and G4 needs the same for $\chi$. Fix: say "for linearly independent $\psi_1,\psi_2$" and "assuming $\varphi$ smooth". Optional: give the exact Wigner argument "$U(t)=U(t/2)^2$, and the square of an antiunitary map is unitary" in place of "a continuous path cannot switch type".
- [minor] "With $M=0$ no normalizable states localized in position exist" — the wording misleads, because with $M\neq0$ no normalizable state is localized at a point either. The real result is about localizability (no position observable) in $M=0$ representations, and it has no citation. Fix: "with $M=0$ there is no position operator, so no localizable particle states (Inönü & Wigner 1952) [theorem, cited]". Add that reference to the §4 Sources line.
- [minor] Conditions left unstated: (i) Stone–von Neumann needs irreducibility (one spinless particle). (ii) G6: requiring $[K,H]=i\hbar P$ (velocity $=P/M$) inside the field also rules out vector potentials. The general external-field form is $(P-A(X))^2/2M+W(X)$ (Ballentine §3.4), so "local potential" should say "scalar". (iii) "Poincaré gives Klein–Gordon or Dirac" is overstated. Poincaré gives Wigner's classification, and KG and Dirac are the covariant equations for spin 0 and ½, with single-particle caveats.
- [minor] Sources. §1 cites Sakurai & Napolitano §2.7, which covers gravity/COW only. In the 2nd edition the 4π neutron experiment is in §3.2 and spin magnetic resonance is in §5.5, so add both. Weinberg *Lectures* §3.5 is space translation; time translation and $H$ are in the following section, so check §3.5/§3.6 and where Galilean invariance is treated.
