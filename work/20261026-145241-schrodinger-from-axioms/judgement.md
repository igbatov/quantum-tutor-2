B's Galilean part (G2 to G6) uses four cited theorems (Wigner, the M=0 no-localized-states result, maximal commuting set, Stone–von Neumann). - B has the real counterweight. ## Notes for the learner
- A (Boosted matter waves): suits you if you want to check every line yourself, and the experiments (Davisson–Germer, COW) feed directly into the derivation. Weakest point: it gets H=p²/2m by adding de Broglie and classical velocity addition as assumptions. So it is "Galilean invariance plus the classical limit", not Galilean invariance alone.
- B (Spinning two-state matrices): suits you if you want the standard Ballentine/Bargmann route, with mass emerging as the central charge and p=-iħ∇ derived from commutators, plus a fully multiplied-out 2×2 example. Weakest point: the Galilean section rests on several cited theorems and compresses steps you will want expanded. The spin experiments (Rabi, 4π rotation) motivate U(t) but not the particle Hamiltonian.
