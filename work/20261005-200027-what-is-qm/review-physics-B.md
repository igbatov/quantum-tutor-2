VERDICT: revise

## Issues
- [major] "blocks light wiggling straight across it" / "passes another vertical filter for certain" — this is an unnamed idealization. Paragraphs 2–4 and the 30° example all rely on ideal filters, but "assuming ideal filters" first appears in the three-filter paragraph. The text names sunglasses as its example, and real polarizers like those don't fully block crossed light. Nor do they pass every aligned photon. So "blocks", "for certain" and "passes every one" (in the superposition paragraph) are false as written for the filters the learner has in mind. Fix: state the idealization the first time a filter appears. For example: "An ideal polarizing filter (real ones, like those in sunglasses, come close) passes light wiggling along its axis, blocks light wiggling straight across it, ..." Then keep "ideal" in scope wherever the text says "for certain", "none" or "every one".
- [major] "when you dim the light to single photons" — dimming ordinary light (a lamp or laser) does not produce single photons. It makes photons arrive one at a time *on average*, at random moments, and now and then two arrive together. Fix: "when you dim the light so far that photons arrive one at a time (or use a source built to emit single photons)".
- [major] "turning smoothly in time between measurements as the Schrödinger equation describes" — this holds only for an isolated system, and the condition isn't stated. A system that interacts with its surroundings, without anyone measuring it, can become entangled with them. It then no longer has an arrow of its own (the closing line about entanglement hints at this). As written, the sentence suggests that only "measurements" interrupt smooth evolution, and that is a picture the learner would later have to unlearn when meeting decoherence. Fix: "turning smoothly in time, as the Schrödinger equation describes, as long as the system is left undisturbed and isolated".
- [minor] "updates: the answer you get replaces the arrow" — this is the rule for an ideal measurement that leaves the photon intact, like the filter that passes it. Many real measurements destroy the photon. A blocked photon is absorbed, and a detector click absorbs it too, so no arrow is left to replace. Fix: "updates: if the photon survives the question, its arrow is replaced by the answer it gave".
- [minor] "A photon's polarization is exactly what quantum computers call a qubit" — the word "exactly" reads as if a qubit *is* polarization. In fact polarization is one physical example of a qubit; most quantum computers use other systems. Fix: "A photon's polarization is one example of what quantum computers call a qubit: any system with two basic answers obeying these rules."
- [minor] "Bright light behaves as a wave whose electric field wiggles in a direction" — this describes linearly polarized light. Sunlight and lamp light are unpolarized: their wiggle direction changes randomly. Light whose wiggle goes round in circles (which the text mentions later) has no single direction. Fix: "Light is a wave whose electric field wiggles across the beam; when it wiggles back and forth along one fixed line, that line is its polarization."
- [minor] "what physically happens when it updates, are still debated" — this assumes some physical update happens. Some interpretations (e.g. many-worlds) deny that any collapse occurs. Fix: "what, if anything, physically happens when it updates".

## Sound points worth keeping
- The superposition paragraph is excellent and correct. It argues "secretly vertical or horizontal" against the 45° filter passing every photon, and it rejects "both at once" in favour of "one arrow pointing one way". The uncertainty trade-off is framed through preparation ("no way of preparing a photon makes both answers certain"), not as measurement clumsiness.
- The three-filter argument is handled honestly. It says plainly that bright light does the same under wave optics and that the quantum part is "photon by photon, by chance". The figures use expected counts, label the ideal filters, and the numbers are correct (cos 30° ≈ 0.866, 0.75 + 0.25 = 1, 100 → 50 → 25). "Nobody needs to watch; the filter does the asking" correctly defuses the conscious-observer idea.
