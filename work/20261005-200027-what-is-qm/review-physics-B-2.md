## Issues
- [major] "a wave that wiggles sideways as it travels, as a shaken rope does" — the rope analogy states only one limit: light "needs no rope or any other material". It doesn't say what wiggles. In a rope the material itself moves sideways. In light nothing moves sideways: the electric field (the push the light would give an electric charge) changes its strength and direction, while the light travels in a straight line. The earlier draft said "electric field", and the rewrite dropped it. Read with the later "photon polarized along a line" and "wiggle goes round in circles", the sentence invites the common, lasting picture of light or photons snaking (or spiralling) sideways along their path. Fix: "...as a shaken rope does, with two differences: light needs no rope or any other material, and nothing actually moves sideways. What wiggles is the electric field, the sideways push the light would give an electric charge, while the light itself goes straight ahead."
- [minor] "States are described by arrows of length 1." — this is true for a whole system with a definite preparation (a pure state). It is not true for every system: the closing line itself says an entangled photon has no arrow of its own, and the same goes for unpolarized light. Fix: "A system's state, when it has one of its own, is described by an arrow of length 1" (this also sets up the entanglement line).
- [minor] "(light whose wiggle goes round in circles already needs this)" — "this" can be read as both "more directions" and "a richer kind". Circularly polarized light needs only the richer kind of arrow, not more directions; it still has just two basic answers. Fix: "...and of a richer kind than the ones drawn here; light whose wiggle goes round in circles already needs the richer kind, though not more directions."

Status of round-one issues: all seven are fixed. These were: ideal filters named at first use and kept in scope; dim light "almost always one at a time"; Schrödinger evolution limited to isolated systems; the update rule limited to ideal measurements that leave the system intact; the qubit called "one example"; polarization defined as wiggling "along one line"; and "what, if anything, physically happens".

The figures show cos 30° ≈ 0.866 and sin 30° = 0.5, the chances 0.75 + 0.25 = 1, and the counts 100 → 50 → 25 as "expected photon counts, ideal filters".
