## Notes for the learner
- A (experiment-first, double slit): suits you if you want to see the strange core of quantum mechanics (chances, interference, what "observing" means) through one experiment. Weakest point: the dark-stripe paragraph is dense, and you have to take the "add amplitudes, then square" rule on trust.
- C (classical-contrast, the atom): suits you if you want to know why quantum mechanics matters: why atoms are stable, identical and glow in sharp colors. Weakest point: it says less about measurement and interference, and the "squeezing raises energy" step is stated rather than shown.

## Notes for revision
- A: In "Take a spot in the first dark stripe...", state the takeaway first ("opening a second slit made some spots unreachable"), then give the details. After "squaring the size of that total", add a two-line number example: +1 and -1 give 0; +1 and +1 give 2, squared 4, versus 1 + 1 = 2 when chances add.
- C: Show the step "a wave squeezed into less space wiggles more sharply, which means more energy" with a picture or the guitar string (a shorter string gives a higher note) rather than just asserting it. Tie "only certain patterns are steady" back to the amplitudes introduced just before, so the guitar analogy isn't carrying the whole idea.
- B (if shown): Define polarization more gently, without "electric field", and cut the jargon from the zoom-out bullets (complex numbers, Schrödinger equation) or define each term in a few words.
