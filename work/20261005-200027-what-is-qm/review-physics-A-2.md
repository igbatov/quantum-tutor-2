## Issues
- [critical] "The stripes vanish, leaving one broad hump." (also "the hump's peak") — False, and it is the exact example section 0 names. A single slit gives a bright central band with fainter side bands. The file's own figure contradicts the text: P1 = sinc²(πX/4) is zero at X = ±4 and has a side band from 4 to 6 inside the plotted window. Fix: "Now close one slit. The fine stripes are replaced by one wide bright band in the middle (with much fainter bands further out)." Then use "the peak of that band" instead of "the hump's peak".
- [major] "the two one-slit humps simply added: the smooth spread you first guessed" — False on two counts. In the file's own model the which-slit result is 2·P1: one central band, not two humps, and it still has the single-slit dark gaps at X = ±4 and faint side bands, so it is not "smooth". Fix: "the stripes disappear and you get the one-slit patterns simply added: one wide band with no fine stripes, as if chances just add."
- [major] "If each electron simply went through one slit or the other, opening the second slit could only add hits" — Holds only under an unstated assumption: an electron passing one slit is unaffected by whether the other slit is open. As written it rules out the pilot-wave picture that paragraph 28 presents as viable (a single definite path steered by both routes' amplitudes), so the text contradicts itself and treats interpretations unevenly. The figure's curve-2 label has the same problem. Fix: "If each electron went through one slit and was unaffected by whether the other was open, opening the second slit could only add hits." Relabel curve 2 "if chances simply added (also the result with a which-slit detector)".
- [major] "made the spot unreachable" / figure: "curve 3 is exactly zero" labelled "what is actually seen" — Unnamed idealization. Exact zeros hold only for ideal slits, a perfectly monochromatic and coherent beam, and perfect detector resolution. Real data have small nonzero minima, which the text itself admits elsewhere ("almost none", "essentially never"). Fix: "made the spot almost unreachable". Label curve 3 "both slits open (ideal set-up)", and the caption should say that in an ideal set-up curve 3 drops to zero, and in real experiments to nearly zero.
- [major] "Even one stray photon bouncing off the electron is enough if it carries away which-slit information" (with "The stripes disappear") — True only for a complete, reliable record. A photon that carries partial which-slit information (for example, wavelength not short compared with the slit separation) only fades the stripes. Fix: "If the record reliably tells the slits apart, the stripes disappear; a fuzzy record only fades them. Even one stray photon can be such a record, and nobody ever has to read it."
- [major] "so the two add to silence" — False as written. Real noise-cancelling headphones reduce noise, mainly at low frequencies, and never produce silence. Fix: "so the two largely cancel and you hear much less noise."
- [minor] "the theory of how matter and light behave at the scale of atoms" — Reads as if the theory only applies at atomic scale, yet paragraph 26 relies on it applying to big objects. Fix: "the theory of how matter and light behave; its effects are most obvious at the scale of atoms and smaller."
- [minor] "electrons land there nearly as often as at the hump's peak" — True only for slits much narrower than their separation (the figure assumes separation = 4 × width). Fix: add "with slits much narrower than the gap between them".

## Sound points worth keeping
- "Observing" is defined as any physical record, not a mind, and the text notes nobody has to read the record. "Two places at once" is correctly flagged as going beyond the theory.
- The figure normalizations are consistent: the both-slits curve averages to the which-slit curve, it is 2× higher at the centre, and P1 is about 0.95 and 0.62 at the marked dark stripes. The amplitude analogy states its limits.
