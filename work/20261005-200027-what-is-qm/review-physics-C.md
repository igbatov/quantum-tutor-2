## Issues
- [minor] "this trade-off is the uncertainty principle at work" — Mildly overstated. The squeeze-vs-pull balance is the standard heuristic and is right in spirit. A rigorous proof that hydrogen is stable uses a related but stronger wave inequality, not Heisenberg's relation itself. Fix: "this trade-off is closely related to the uncertainty principle, and it is a property of waves, not of clumsy measurement."
- [minor] "the physics that explains what ordinary matter is" — The opening casts QM mainly as the theory of atoms, which could suggest it applies only to small things or only to matter. Line 27's "anything trapped" partly corrects this. Fix: add a clause such as "it applies to everything, but its effects are most obvious at the scale of atoms."
- [minor] "how many different colors of light, at most" — Some drops (for example rung 2 to rung 1 in hydrogen) produce ultraviolet, which isn't a visible color. The answer of 3 still holds, but a careful learner might hesitate over invisible light. Fix: "how many different kinds of light (colors, including invisible ones), at most…"

## Sound points worth keeping
- The numbers are correct: classical collapse time of about 10^-11 s, the -13.6/n^2 eV ladder, Balmer lines at 656.3/486.1/434.0/410.2 nm, and Lyman-alpha in the UV. The guitar-string analogy has its limits stated (nothing material vibrates; the rungs crowd together rather than being evenly spaced).
- It handles the classic traps well. The Born rule is stated plainly and "each look finds the whole electron". The ground state is described as a round ball with no orbit that still has motion. The uncertainty principle is framed as a wave property and not as measurement disturbance. What happens between looks is left to the interpretations, and the closing line is even-handed.
