## Issues
- [major] "(for clock-hand pieces, turning each hand on the spot): jumps, not turns" (account 2). This is false for the clock-hand case. Turning each hand by a phase is a continuous family: it can be done by as little as you like. The true statement is that for p ≠ 2 none of the allowed changes moves any chance from one exit to another, except by a sudden relabelling. Fix: "...swapping the labels of the directions and reversing signs (for clock-hand pieces, also turning each hand on the spot, which can be done gradually but never shifts any chance between exits). None of these moves photons gradually from one exit to the other; only a sudden swap does."
- [major] "Everything in this section is contested." This is false as written, and the section contradicts it. It contains items labelled "A mathematical fact" (pilot-wave equivariance) and a theorem (Gisin's no-signalling result). Both are uncontested. So is the agreement of all four on every count. A learner would conclude that those results are disputed. Fix: "Which interpretation is right, and whether each one's account of the square succeeds, is contested; the mathematical results quoted along the way are not."
- [minor] "it is forced by consistency once states are turnable arrows" (final summary). This compresses away the conditions the text itself states. With only two directions, account 1's wiggle rule is consistent and survives every turn. Turnability alone forces the square only among powers. Fix: "among powers, it is forced once states are turnable arrows; among all rules, it is forced once there are three or more directions and chances don't depend on grouping".
- [minor] "the only chance rules satisfying this are the squared shadow of the arrow" (account 3). The opening's "the only rule of any kind" and the summary's "no rule other than the squared shadow survives at all" have the same problem. On its own, Gleason gives squared shadows of *some* arrow, or averages of them. Tying the rule to *the* prepared arrow needs the "passes every time" fact, which account 3 adds one clause later. Fix: in account 3, say "squared shadows of some arrow, or averages of them"; in the opening and summary, add "(given that a photon prepared along a direction always passes it)" or "up to mixtures".
- [minor] "for a typical starting condition of the universe the square holds anyway" (pilot-wave). The "typical" in Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì is itself measured by the squared size of the universal wave function. That is the circularity critics point to, and "anyway" hides it. Fix: "for a starting condition that is typical, judged by the squared size of the universe's wave function, the square holds for subsystems".
