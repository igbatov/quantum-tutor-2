## Notes for the learner
- A (Pythagoras at the polarizer): best if you want the reason in one picture: a right triangle where only squared shadows add up to all the photons, then three accounts of rising rigour. Its weakest point is that it switches from electrons to photons and leans on some unexplained lab terms (wave plate, heralded photons, Stern–Gerlach).
- C (three slits, add and subtract): best if you want a decisive experiment: the square predicts "no three-way interference", other powers don't, and the lab found none. Its weakest point is that the *why* (Pythagoras, Gleason) comes late, after a long fine-print detour.
