## Issues
- [critical] "has to use the square" (account 2); also the opening's "the square is the only rule" and "has no other option" in "How the accounts relate". This is false as written, and account 1's own counterexample breaks it. The wiggle rule depends only on a component's size, so it is unchanged by every turn. One example is chance = (shadow)² + ε·sin(2π·shadow²). Its pass and reflect chances total exactly 1 for every arrow and every angle, and any turn keeps that true. The turning theorem only rules out other *powers* p. It does not rule out non-power rules, so account 2 does not "close the gap" that account 1 left. Only Gleason does that, and only with three or more directions plus non-contextuality. Fix: in the opening, say "once amplitudes are shadows of an arrow, the square is the only power under which the two exits always add up to all the photons; with three or more outcomes, and chances that don't depend on grouping, it is the only rule of any kind". In account 2, say "among power rules, a world whose amplitudes add, cancel and can be turned a little at a time has to use p = 2 (non-integer p included)". At the end of account 1, say "closing that gap takes account 3". In "How the accounts relate", say "no other power".
- [major] "the photon comes out polarized at 60° instead" (observation 5). This is true only if the splitter is at 30°, and (5) never says that. In general, the half-wave delay sends a 0° photon out at 2θ. Fix: "With the splitter at 30°, lengthen one path by half a wavelength and the photon comes out at 60° (in general at twice the splitter angle)…". Then "the 60° after a sign reversal" in Model 1 also reads correctly.
- [major] "heralded photons whose polarization is set by their partner's polarizer". This states a collapse-style, remote-causation reading as fact, which is a trap given the no-signalling theme. It also misdescribes the cited experiment. Dehlinger & Mitchell (2002) measure coincidence rates of entangled pairs with a polarizer in each arm. They do not run the set-up described here (a heralded photon sent through a 0° polarizer). Fix: "(The same cos² dependence is seen in the coincidence counts of entangled photon pairs with a polarizer in each arm, e.g. Dehlinger and Mitchell, 2002.)" Or cite a direct heralded single-photon polarizer run instead.
- [major] "Bell pointed out in 1966 that experiment does not force it". This misapplies Bell. His 1966 objection to Gleason was about hidden-variable *value assignments*: the outcome of an individual run might depend on the whole arrangement. It was not about quantum *probabilities*. For the observed frequencies, the chance of a shared outcome is the same in every grouping that has been tested. What experiment cannot do is check every possible grouping. Fix: "Critics note that the assumption does most of the work: it is checked for the groupings tested, but assumed for all of them. (Bell, 1966, made the related point that for hidden variables an individual outcome could depend on the whole arrangement of the apparatus.)"
- [major] "measured to a few per cent" (final summary). This understates the experimental status. "A few per cent" is the agreement of the single-photon runs described here. Dedicated tests of the square rule's consequences go further. For example, three-path ("triple-slit") tests look for interference beyond pairs, which the square forbids. Sinha et al., *Science* 329, 418 (2010) bounded it at about the 1% level, and later work went tighter. Fix: "the runs described here agree to a few per cent, and dedicated tests such as three-slit experiments find no departure at the per-cent level or below".
- [minor] "Born borrowed the square from that analogy". This is stated as historical fact. It rests on Born's own later recollection (Einstein's idea that light intensity gives photon density). Also, Born's follow-up 1926 paper (*Z. Phys.* 38, 803) supported the square with a normalization argument, so "put in because it fits" is incomplete. Fix: "by Born's own later account, he was guided by that analogy".
- [minor] "the chance that the wave settles at a place is its squared size there". In GRW/CSL the collapse centre is weighted by the squared size averaged over the localization width (about 100 nm). Fix: add "(averaged over a tiny region)".
- [minor] "Bell tests rule out every rule in which each photon's exit depends only…". This holds only under Bell's other assumptions, especially that the splitter settings are chosen independently of the pointers. Fix: add "given that the settings are chosen independently of the pointers".

## Sound points worth keeping
- Account 1's explicit "only among plain powers" caveat and the wiggle counterexample. The Gleason statement is correct: dimension ≥ 3, non-contextuality, density-operator mixtures, and pure-state recovery via "passes every time". Busch 2003 is correctly scoped to unsharp (POVM) measurements in dimension 2. The arithmetic and the hidden-pointer line (67/50/33%) check out.
- The interpretations are labelled contested and treated evenly. Pilot-wave equivariance is correctly separated, as "a mathematical fact", from the contested typicality argument. The citations checked (Born footnote, GRA 1986, Thorn 2004, Gleason 1957, Busch 2003, Zurek 2003, DGZ 1992, Valentini–Westman 2005, Gisin 1989) are correct.
