## Issues
- [critical] "the record spreads into more and more particles ... product of all the individual overlaps" — This mixes up two different processes. The overlap of the two environment states changes only through interactions with the molecule itself. Once the gas atom carries the tag, its later collisions with the wall and with other atoms happen inside the environment. Those collisions leave the overlap exactly unchanged, because unitary evolution preserves overlaps. Spreading makes sorting impossible in practice. It does not weaken the stripes any further. The product rule holds for N particles that each scatter off the molecule independently, and N collisions is what Hornberger et al. varied through the pressure. Figure a-leaked-tag has the same error: its axis is labelled "the number of particles the tag has spread into". Fix: "Each further gas atom that hits the molecule adds its own tag, and the overlaps multiply: fifty weak taggers at 0.9 each leave stripes about 0.5% strong. Separately, each tag then spreads through later collisions in the surroundings. That does not change the overlap, but it scatters the record over so many particles that sorting it out again is impossible in practice." Relabel the figure axis "N, the number of particles that have each scattered off the molecule (independent)". Give spreading its own sentence in the caption.
- [major] "it sat in one photon with a handle on it, a polarizer" / "left-turning and right-turning are the two arrows, the two diagonal polarizations are the halfway directions" — In the Walborn experiment the slit photon is entangled, so its polarization on its own is random. After the plates, the tag state for slit 1 is ∝ L_s V_p + R_s H_p and for slit 2 it is ∝ R_s V_p + L_s H_p. These are orthogonal, so the stripes go. But the slit photon's polarization alone says nothing about the slit: the which-path information sits in the joint polarization of the pair. The partner's ±45° polarizer measures the partner. It does not measure the slit photon's L/R along the diagonals. For a lone H-polarized photon tagged L/R, the halfway directions would be H and V of that same photon. The correct mechanism: "partner passed +45°" means the slit photon arrived diagonally polarized. Diagonal light lies along the plates' axes, so it gets no tag, only a quarter-wave relative delay. So with these settings each group's stripes sit a quarter spacing from the untagged stripes. That also makes Model 2's "they cancel where they did without a tag" wrong for this experiment, as the a-same-situation caption half-admits. Fix: say the tag is in the pair's joint polarization. Explain erasure as "the partner's diagonal result picks out pairs whose slit photon was diagonal, which the plates leave untagged". Drop "sat in one photon" and "cancel where they did without a tag", or label that phrase as the idealized case.
- [major] "whichever direction you choose to measure the partner's polarization along ... that same direction" — This is false for the state Walborn et al. used, (|H⟩|V⟩ + |V⟩|H⟩)/√2. Along H/V the two photons always disagree, and along the diagonals (and circular) they always agree. But at a general linear angle θ, such as 22.5°, the partner's result puts the slit photon at 90° − θ. So it does not fix the slit photon's result along θ. Fix: "measured along horizontal/vertical, the two photons always give opposite results; measured along the two diagonals, always the same." That is all the text and the check question use.
- [major] "Then sorting by the partner could only pick subsets of the same stripe-free pattern, never stripes" — This contradicts (c), where subsets of the stripe-free pattern (b) are striped. The argument needs its premise stated. Fix: "If each photon used one slit and landed according to that slit's own one-slit chances, whatever its partner later does, then any group picked by the partner would be a mixture of the two one-slit patterns, and a mixture of those has no stripes. (c) shows striped groups, so that premise fails." This also makes the contrast with pilot-wave clean: its landing spot does depend on the wave through both slits.
- [major] "The record that kills the stripes does not have to touch the particle" — Both tags are written by interactions with the particle: microwaves acting on the atom's internal state, and wave plates acting on the photon's polarization. No record can form without an interaction. The experiments show that the record need not push the particle off course. Fix: "does not have to push the particle off course". Also check figure a-ruled-out row 1 and the "kick-free" wording against this.
- [major] "fades the stripes in proportion to how well its two end states can be told apart" / "the result is a coin toss whichever slit was used" — The first is false as written: V = √(1 − D²), which is not proportional. D = 0.87 still leaves V = 0.5. Fix: "fades the stripes more the better its end states can be told apart, within the budget V² + D² ≤ 1". The second holds only for a perfect tag (θ = 90°). At θ = 60°, the case drawn in a-arrows-budget, the halfway and perpendicular results come with chances cos²30° = 0.75 and sin²30° = 0.25. These are the same for both slits, so they still reveal nothing about the slit, but they are not a coin toss. Fix: "gives the same chances whichever slit was used (a coin toss for a perfect tag), so it reveals nothing about the slit".
- [major] "so erasure works exactly as in standard theory" / "the tests are the large-molecule experiments" — Collapse models give every particle a tiny nonzero collapse rate (photons excepted in GRW/CSL), so "exactly" is false. Say "indistinguishably from standard theory: the difference is far below anything measurable". The tests are also not only interferometric. Searches for spontaneous heating and spontaneous X-ray emission (for example, the underground test that ruled out the parameter-free Diósi–Penrose model, Donadi et al. 2020) now set many of the strongest bounds. Fix: "the tests include large-molecule interferometry and searches for spontaneous heating and radiation, which so far find no departure."

## Sound points worth keeping
- The Dürr–Nonn–Rempe tagging mechanism is accurate: hyperfine states about 3 GHz apart, a state-dependent sign flip on the deflected part only, and a microwave-to-optical momentum ratio of about 10⁻⁵. The text says decoherence does not explain the single dot.
- V = cos θ and D = sin θ (Helstrom) for pure, equal-weight tags; 0.9⁵⁰ ≈ 0.005 is correct. The interpretations are treated even-handedly, with no-signalling stated correctly.
