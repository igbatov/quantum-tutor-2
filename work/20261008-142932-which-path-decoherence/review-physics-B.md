## Issues
- [major] "that is why large things are found at places rather than in superpositions" — This overclaims for decoherence. Decoherence explains why superpositions of places stop showing interference and look like "one place, unknown which". It does not explain why a thing is *found* at one place. The draft says this itself in its last sections, so the two passages contradict each other. Fix: "that is why, for large things, superpositions of different places show no interference and behave like 'at one place, unknown which'; why one place is found is the separate question at the end."
- [major] "'big things don't interfere because they are big' (molecules of two thousand atoms do" — The draft presents as settled what is open. Objective-collapse models say exactly that size itself eventually matters, and the draft's own collapse paragraph says tests are probing this. Fix: "'big things can't interfere because they are big': as far as experiments go (up to about two thousand atoms), what matters is the wavelength and what the surroundings copy; whether size itself matters for much larger objects is what collapse tests probe."
- [major] "the resolution was that the two bookkeepings agree whenever the record comes with a kick" — This misstates Wiseman & Harrison. Their point was that a which-path record always changes the atom's momentum distribution, a quantum, nonlocal momentum transfer, but not as a classical random kick. So both sides were partly right, and the semantics stayed debated (Wiseman et al. 1997; Dürr & Rempe 2000). Also, "the stripes vanished completely" is an absolute about an experiment. Fix: "the resolution was that a record always changes the atom's momentum distribution, but not necessarily by classical random kicks; where there is no such kick (1998), the stripes still disappeared, to within the experiment's precision." Keep "the record is what matters" as the mainstream reading, but don't call the question closed.
- [major] "a few-photon field in a cavity left in two states at once" — This is the "two places at once" trap, applied to states. Fix: "a few-photon field put into a superposition of two fields of different phase".
- [major] "near 1 for pairs the surroundings cannot resolve and near 0 beyond" (caption of b-pairs-of-routes) — This is false for the plotted 1-scattering-time curve. With the stated formula, beyond about λ/2 the factor is exp(−1) ≈ 0.37, not near 0. Fix: "falls to exp(−number of scatterings) beyond about half a wavelength: about 0.37 after one scattering time, effectively 0 after ten".
- [major] "identical predictions for every experiment above in all but the last" — This is false as written. Objective collapse also agrees, to within unmeasurable amounts, with every experiment described. The draft's own figure line ("same predictions for every experiment above; collapse differs only for much larger objects") is the correct version. Fix: use the figure line in the text.
- [minor] "each atom absorbs a photon ... about one photon per atom on average" — "Each" and "on average" contradict each other. Atoms that scatter no photon keep their stripes, so the measured dip is shallower than the ideal one-photon curve. Also, the revival past λ/2 comes back with the stripes shifted by half a spacing (the overlap is negative there; Chapman et al. measured this phase jump), and |sin x/x| in b-one-photon hides that. Fix: "most atoms scatter one photon (some none, some two)". Add to the caption: "beyond d = λ/2 the revived stripes are shifted by half a spacing".
- [minor] Sources. Englert, Scully & Walther, *Nature* 375, 367 (1995) "Complementarity and uncertainty": matches what I know. Feynman & Hibbs ch. 12 ("Other problems in probability", which contains the influence functional): matches, but give no section number unless checked. Joos et al. ch. 3 (Joos's chapter): correct for the scattering estimates. The dust-grain numbers are consistent with Joos–Zeh: about 1 s for the microwave background at Δx = 10 µm (Λ ≈ 10^6 cm⁻² s⁻¹), about 10^18 air impacts/s, about 10^11 sunlight photons/s. Schlosshauer ch. 3, 5 and 6 by topic are right; check the exact title of ch. 8 before quoting it. Problems: (a) Zurek, *Physics Today* 1991 is cited as "the popular version with the table of times". As far as I know, its estimate is the Brownian-motion formula τ_D = τ_R(λ_th/Δx)², not the Joos–Zeh scattering table, so describe it as such. (b) The page ranges (Schlosshauer pp. 115–151, "from p. 13"; Maudlin pp. 94–136, 137–172, 173–204) can't be confirmed from what I know. The "About the sources" note claims they were confirmed. Unless that check really happened, drop the pages and keep chapter titles (request.md: pages only if confident). The Bell chapters (ch. 20 p. 181, ch. 23 p. 213, 2nd ed.) match what I know.
