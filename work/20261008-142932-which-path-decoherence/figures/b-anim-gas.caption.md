Experiment 2 (2003), animated: argon is let in step by step, from pressure 0 to 3 p₀ in steps of 0.5 p₀, where p₀ is the pressure at which the stripe visibility has fallen to 1/e. Panel A is a schematic, not to scale. It shows C70 molecules, drawn as dots, flying from the oven through three gratings to the detector. The number of grey argon dots drawn is proportional to the pressure. A star marks a collision. After a collision a molecule either keeps going (open circle: it now carries a record and its stripes are gone) or is knocked out of the beam (cross). The stripes themselves can't be seen in flight. Panels B and C are computed from one stated model. Collisions are independent, so their number is random, with a mean proportional to the pressure (2 per molecule at p₀). One collision is a complete record (overlap 0), and that is the theory's reading of the experiment. In an illustrative assumption, not a measured value, half of all collisions knock the molecule out. With these rules the visibility among the molecules that arrive is exp(−p/p₀), and the arriving fraction also falls as exp(−p/p₀). Panel B shows the count behind the third grating as it is slid sideways, idealized as a sinusoid with period 1 µm and perfect stripes at zero pressure (the real zero-pressure visibility was lower). The solid curve is the actual count. The dash-dot curve divides out the lost molecules, so you can see that the stripes fade among the molecules that do arrive, not just because fewer arrive. Panel C plots visibility against pressure on a logarithmic vertical axis, where the exponential is a straight line: each equal pressure step cuts the visibility by the same factor (about 0.61 per 0.5 p₀). No data are drawn. What to notice: in panel A the arriving molecules that were never hit (filled dots) become rare as the open circles take over, and that fraction is exactly what panel B's stripe depth measures. Nobody detects the gas atoms.
