What remains:

## Issues
- [major] Figure b-does-it-explain-the-dot, many-worlds box: "both dots happen, in two branches that can no longer interfere". This is a counting error. With no stripes, each path-part still spreads over the whole screen, so there are not two possible dots. Every possible dot position occurs, each in its own branch. The figure suggests the two paths give two dots, one per path, which is the "two places" picture that has to be unlearned. Fix: "every possible dot happens, each in its own branch; the branches can no longer interfere; we are in one".
- [major] "a which-path record always changes the spread of the atom's sideways momentum". This is false as written. For two path-parts that don't overlap, the momentum spread (variance) is the same with or without stripes, because the cross term adds nothing to ⟨p²⟩. What always changes is the shape of the momentum distribution, and that is Wiseman & Harrison's point. The parenthesis "(on a far screen, the stripes are that spread)" repeats the error. Fix: "always changes the atom's sideways momentum distribution (on a far screen, the pattern is that distribution), but not necessarily by anything like a classical random kick".
- [major] "grows with pressure, time or temperature, the visibility falls exponentially". The visibility is exponential in the mean number of scatterings, so it is exponential in pressure or in time at fixed conditions. It is not exponential in temperature. In Experiment 3 both the photon rate (×~600) and each photon's effectiveness change steeply with T. Fix: "...falls exponentially with the mean number of scatterings, so exponentially in pressure (Experiment 2) or in time; with temperature it falls more steeply still, because hotter molecules emit both more photons and photons that record more".
- [major] "Testing collapse means doing what Experiments 2 and 3 show is hard". This absolute is false. Non-interferometric tests exist: spontaneous heating of cold masses and cantilevers, spontaneous X-ray emission, and LISA Pathfinder bounds. They are reviewed in the Bassi et al. paper the text itself cites. Fix: "One way to test collapse is what Experiments 2 and 3 show is hard...; another is to look for the faint heating or radiation the random localizations would cause in ordinary matter."
- [minor] Model 1: "a number from 1 ... to 0, with the curve of Experiment 1 in between". The overlap itself goes negative on that curve, and the b-one-photon caption says so; only its size runs from 1 to 0. Model 2's factor "scales the cancelling" is in general complex too, so it also shifts the stripes. Fix: "its size runs from 1 ... to 0" and "a factor (in general complex: it can also shift the stripes) whose size measures...".
- [minor] Three figure labels are slightly off. In b-rules-out, panel (b) is titled "seen when one photon is scattered", but the curves are computed; the caption correctly says "what the model gives", so retitle it "model: one photon scattered". The b-rules-out footnote "whenever the record comes with a recoil" is broader than true; it holds when the recoil and the record are the same particle's momentum, as in photon scattering. Use "for photon scattering". The b-does-it-explain-the-dot bottom line "collapse differs only for much larger objects" should read "collapse differs detectably only for much larger objects", to match the caption and text.
- [minor] "Copenhagen-style, many-worlds and pilot-wave use that theory unchanged". Pilot-wave keeps the wave equation unchanged but adds particle positions and a guidance law, and the text itself says so earlier. Fix: "keep that theory's wave equation and predictions unchanged (pilot-wave adds particle positions)".
