## Issues
- [major] "chances over any division of the screen total 1, then ... squared length" (Model 3, Cons). False as written. Divisions of the screen are a commuting family of projections, and Gleason says nothing about a single commuting family. For example, chance(R) = ∫_R|ψ|⁴ / ∫|ψ|⁴ depends only on the state and the region and sums to 1 over every division of the screen, yet it is not |ψ|². Gleason needs additivity over every complete set of mutually exclusive outcomes the state space allows, including measurements that are not screen regions. The closing table states this correctly ("every complete set"), so the two statements contradict each other. Fix: "...and if chances are additive and total 1 over every complete set of perpendicular outcomes the formalism allows (every orthonormal basis, not only the ways of dividing the screen), with each outcome's chance independent of which set it is measured in, then the chance of R must be the squared length..."
- [major] "all give identical predictions for every count" (header of the closing table). False for one row of the same table. GRW objective collapse predicts departures from standard quantum mechanics: loss of interference for large masses, and spontaneous heating. The row itself says "predicted departures not found so far". Fix: "the first six give identical predictions for every count; objective collapse agrees with the Born frequencies here but predicts small departures elsewhere, not yet observed."
- [major] "After $N$ electrons there are $2^N$ branches, one per string" (also "each arrival having split it in two" in the opening). This counting claim is false in the reading being described. A position-sensitive detector records where the dot lands, so each arrival decoheres into many branches, one for each distinguishable recorded position, and "in/out" is a grouping of them. The point matters because the count-versus-weight argument in Cons depends on it, and the fine-grained count is not even well defined. That ill-definedness is itself one of the standard objections to counting branches. Fix: "Grouping each arrival's recorded positions into 'in' and 'out', there are $2^N$ coarse branches, one per string. The number of fine-grained branches depends on how finely one distinguishes records and is not well defined, which is part of why counting fails."
- [major] "standard deviation ... $\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}$, the same expression as the floor" (Model 3; also Model 2 Pros "the same expression as the measured $1/\sqrt N$ scatter"). Math error. The floor of the parabola is $p(1-p)/N$, which is the variance, the square of the standard deviation. It is not the same expression. Fix: "its square, the variance $p(1-p)/N$, is exactly the floor of Model 2's parabola", and in Pros: "the floor $p(1-p)/N$ equals the variance of the frequency, the square of the measured $1/\sqrt N$ scatter, once lengths are read as chances."
- [major] "and nothing about chances was postulated" (Model 2, end of "The limit"). This contradicts the paragraph's own first sentence, which keeps the certainty rule, and Con (i), which calls that rule the probability-1 case of the Born rule. As written it overclaims exactly the point that is in dispute. Fix: "and no chance other than certainty (probability 1) was postulated."
- [major] "They predict the same count for every experiment" (How they relate). This is overstated and contradicts the draft's own "What it does not" paragraph. On their own, Models 1 and 2 give no finite-$N$ probabilities until small weight or small length is read as "rare". Fix: "Wherever they make predictions they agree, so no measurement decides among them. Models 1 and 2 yield finite-$N$ predictions only once small weight is read as rare, which is the step in dispute."
- [major] "by Gleason's theorem any rule respecting the Hilbert-space structure must be $|c|^2$" (supporters' reply). This overstates Gleason, which also needs non-contextuality and at least three dimensions (the draft itself says so in Model 3 and in the table). Attributing this Gleason reply to Hartle, FGG and Gutmann is also not something I can confirm from those papers. Fix: "...and, as others add, Gleason's theorem shows that any non-contextual probability rule (in three or more dimensions) must be $|c|^2$". Name a source only if one is verified.

  - "Dürr, Goldstone & Zanghì 1992" in the table should read Goldstein (the Sources line has it right).
  - Feynman Vol. III §1-5 to §1-7 is cited for the classical pictures being ruled out, but bullets and waves are §1-2 and §1-3. Use §1-2 to §1-5.
  - Griffiths & Schroeter treat "determinate states" in §3.2 (§3.2.2), not §3.4.
  - The caption of c-born-spread says its curves are "1 minus the exact deviant weights of the previous figure". That holds only for ε = 0.05; the previous figure uses ε = 0.1 and 0.05, not 0.01.
  - "fewer electrons land with both sides open than with one" was not measured in the Hitachi biprism run. Attribute it to Bach et al. 2013.
  - In the Hitachi frames, the claim that the share "settled on a definite number" (opening, and observation 3) is a visual inference from two frames, not a measured fraction. Say "appears to settle".

## Sound points worth keeping
- All of the numerical work checks out:
  - the N = 2, 3 and 4 tables and the N = 20 counts and weights (0.588 vs 0.0026);
  - the variance identity and its N = 3 check;
  - the Chebyshev bound and the parabola values at N = 2;
  - the overlap e^{-15.2} for 10^5 copies;
  - f_q and the norm totals, with the N = 20 peaks at 13–14, 16 and 19.
- The handling of status is honest. It names the assumptions (product rule, certainty rule, R1–R3 with a regularity condition). It notes that FGG's construction lives in a non-separable infinite-product space, that the 2 enters through the norm, and that the Squires and Caves–Schack criticisms are labelled and paired with the replies. Every journal reference I checked is correct.
