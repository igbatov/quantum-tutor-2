## Issues
- [major] "No probability was assumed; the exponent came out rather than going in." — This states the supporters' side of the dispute as fact, and the draft's own "Which norm" section contradicts it. There the 2 enters through the squared length, the 2-norm used to decide what is negligible and to define the limit, which is exactly the charge from Squires and from Caves & Schack. A learner reading this sentence first will take the contested claim as settled. Fix: "No probability rule was written in as such. The exponent 2 entered through the squared length used to say which part of the list is negligible and to take the limit (see 'Which norm'). Whether that counts as assuming the Born rule is the dispute."
- [major] "All four predict every count in (1) and (2) identically" — This contradicts the draft itself. Model 1 says the certainty rule "says nothing at all about the fraction at finite N" and has to "borrow" the Born rule for any finite run. So the frequency-operator reading on its own does not predict the scatter of 5 ions in a 100-ion run. Fix: "The Born postulate, many-worlds with squared-entry weights, and pilot-wave theory predict every count in (1) and (2) identically. The frequency-operator argument on its own fixes only the long-run fraction, agrees with them there, and needs the Born reading for finite-N scatter."
- [major] "constrained by Bell tests for pairs" (table row "tickets"; also "it is Bell tests on pairs that constrain them") — The word "local" is missing. Bell tests constrain only local hidden-answer models, given the usual assumptions. Pilot-wave theory, introduced in Model 3 as "one definite string" per run, is a nonlocal hidden-answer theory that Bell tests do not constrain. As written, the learner can conclude that Bell has ruled out pilot-wave theory, which is the classic "Bell rules out all hidden variables" trap. Fix: "local versions are ruled out by Bell tests on pairs (under the usual assumptions); nonlocal ones such as pilot-wave theory are not".
- [minor] "the formalism can assign long-run frequencies to only one exponent, 2" — Opening summary. The draft's own q-norm table shows that this holds only once the length is the 2-norm, and that qualifier is missing here. The closing summary words it correctly ("read with certainty-for-eigenvectors and the squared length"). Fix: "read with the certainty rule and the squared length, the formalism can assign long-run frequencies to only one exponent, 2".
- [minor] "by Gleason's theorem any rule respecting the Hilbert-space structure must be" — Gleason needs non-contextuality and dimension 3 or more. Without those conditions it does not apply, for example to a single two-level ion. It is also not clear that Hartle, FGG and Gutmann actually make this Gleason reply. Fix: "and some add that Gleason's theorem (given non-contextuality, dimension 3 or more) already forces |c|²". Attribute that point only if a source supports it.
- [minor] "Prepare a hundred trapped ions ... That scatter, measured in 1993" — This reads as if Itano et al. ran 100-ion groups at these settings. The figure title "the 1993 ion experiment matched" with N = 10, 100, 1000 curves suggests the same. Fix: give the ion numbers actually used in the paper, or say "illustrated with 100 ions" and "the binomial spread, which the 1993 experiment matched for the group sizes it used".
- [minor] "Supporters of many-worlds themselves largely left ... envariance (Zurek 2003)" — Zurek does not present himself as a many-worlds supporter; envariance belongs to his "existential interpretation". Fix: "...left the frequency route for decision theory (Deutsch 1999; Wallace 2012); a related relative-state approach is Zurek's envariance (2003)."
- [minor] "Curves of this kind: Meekhof et al. 1996" — Meekhof et al. mainly show sideband Rabi flopping of motional states, with several frequencies mixed in, not a clean carrier sin²(Ωt/2). Fix: cite it as "related Rabi-flopping data", or rely on the Leibfried et al. 2003 review for carrier curves.

## Sound points worth keeping
- All the algebra checks out: ⟨F_N⟩ = p, ‖(F_N−λ)Ψ_N‖² = (λ−p)² + p(1−p)/N, the N = 2 hand table, the Chebyshev and Hoeffding numbers, the exact tail ≈ 0.0014, the q-norm table, the 1° overlap example, the branch-count table. The "Which norm" section and the coin-versus-list table are an honest and accurate account of where the 2 enters.
- The citations I checked are accurate (Itano PRA 47, 3554; FGG Ann. Phys. 192, 368; Caves & Schack Ann. Phys. 315, 123, with the correct title; Squires PLA 145, 67; Goldstein & Struyve 2007; Foot chs. 7 and 12; Griffiths §3.2.2 and §3.4). The idealizations behind the experiment are named explicitly.
