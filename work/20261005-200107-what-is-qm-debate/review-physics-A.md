## Issues
- [minor] "Instead of a path, quantum mechanics gives a rule." — This reads as if the theory denies a path, but the closing paragraph correctly lists "whether the electron takes a definite path" as still debated (Bohmian mechanics says it does). The two lines pull against each other slightly. Fix: "Instead of telling us the path, quantum mechanics gives a rule."
- [minor] "Only which outcome gets recorded is left to chance" — Saying it is left to chance treats indeterminism as fundamental, which depends on interpretation (in Bohmian mechanics it is ignorance of initial positions; in many-worlds every outcome occurs). The clause that follows and the final paragraph mostly cover this. Fix: "Only which outcome gets recorded is, as far as anyone can tell, left to chance..."
- [minor] "even 2,000-atom molecules" — True for molecules (Fein et al. 2019). But "even" implies this is the largest case, and I believe metal-cluster interference since 2025 has gone to several thousand atoms. Not wrong. Optional fix: "even molecules of 2,000 atoms, and larger clusters since."

## Sound points worth keeping
- The amplitude-arrow arithmetic is correct and consistent: 1 unit for one slit, 4 for a bright band, 0 for a dark one, an average of 2. The figure formulas (S, 2S, 4S cos²(πx), with the dark band at x = 0.5) agree with the text.
- The traps are handled well. "Observation" means a physical record, the record must reveal the slit, uncertainty is explained through waves rather than disturbance, Bell's result is stated as ruling out local pre-agreed answers only, signalling is ruled out, and interpretations get even treatment with collapse-model tests mentioned. The classical collapse time (~10⁻¹¹ s) is also right.
